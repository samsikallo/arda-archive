The Drawn Sheets

Middle-earth and Beleriand traced one path per pen stroke, drawn into one page at one scale, with the hand that drew each plate named on it
2 plates, 113,737 centrelines, drawn into one page at one scale. Each sheet is traced one path per pen stroke — not shown as a picture — by the same instrument and at the same settings that produced the Marked Sheet. The hand that drew each plate is named on it. The placement is MEASURED, not judged by eye. Each plate's linework was correlated against Middle-earth and Beleriand Map (2) over an exhaustive search of scale and offset, refined to a single pixel: Beleriand at ×0.613, the General Map at ×1.460 offset (792, −257). And the composite SPLICES the two plates rather than superimposing them. Ered Luin is one range and Tolkien drew it on both sheets — so the two drawings ought to coincide — but in this composite Beleriand’s own Ossiriand, Thargelion and Ered Luin were cut away, and what stands east of Lothlann and Estolad is the General Map’s Lindon: the Gulf of Lhûn, Forlindon, Mithlond, Harlindon. The two drawings of the range never meet anywhere on this sheet. The crossover is not an edge but a curve — the ink is Beleriand’s out to about x 780 and the General Map’s beyond x 907. The General Map plate is also a different edition from the one composed here — no ocean hachuring, a square compass cartouche where the reference has a rose, different lettering, and it runs south to Far Harad, which is why its fit is good rather than exact (±5 px, ±0.5%). The lettering is not stripped, and that is a measured refusal: on these plates a mountain range and a word are the same geometry, and the rule that removed one removed the other.
