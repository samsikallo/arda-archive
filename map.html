every named feature the archive draws, and every one of them answers

Hover or focus a feature and its ink turns red; click it for the record. A feature drawn dashed is one whose placing the archive infers rather than reads — the name may still be Tolkien's own. 234 features are drawn here; 219 answer to a record in the gazetteer, and the rest are the draughtsman's own labels. The roads are placed by the place each stage's own label names — never by transforming another map's coordinates onto this one, which measurement showed to be forty-four miles out. A stage the archive cannot place is not drawn, and a course is broken where it cannot be followed: 84 of 88 stages are placed. The chronicle moves through the moments the archive actually records — a place is faint before it was made and dashed after it was thrown down — and a place it cannot date is left alone, because dimming it would assert an absence no text states.

This sheet is a palimpsest: Beleriand is drawn as it was, beneath the pale water that now covers it. For the political extent of the realms age by age — which is drawn in another frame this archive has measured and will not force onto this one — see the realms layer. Christopher Tolkien's own plates are not reproduced here, in linework or in lettering. The archive draws its own map from the corpus and from measurement, records whose hand any original was, and publishes only what its own sources justify; his sheets were traced in the workshop to measure this one against, and those vectors stay there. His sheets keep their own geometry and are shown in their own space: measured against the archive's frame they are 121 miles out across the sheet, so bending them to fit would spoil the one thing they are for. The layer of unmapped places shows 139 records the archive can place and the sheet does not draw — a layer above the map, never drawn into it, because the sheet's restraint is deliberate. A hollow mark is a point borrowed from a containing place or set down by a measure, rather than the place's own.
