# About the quotations

**Why Tolkien's words appear on these pages, how much of them, and what to do if you would
rather they did not.**

Measured and written 26 August 2026. Every figure below was counted from the published
pages themselves, not estimated.

## What this archive is

The Arda Archive is an independent, corpus-first index of J.R.R. Tolkien's legendarium: a
reference work that **cites rather than retells.** Its whole method is that every claim on
a page points back at the line in a book that supports it — and where the books say
nothing, the archive says *nothing was found* instead of filling the gap with something
plausible.

That method requires quotation. A citation a reader cannot check is a citation a reader
has to take on trust, and taking things on trust is the thing this archive exists not to
do. So where the archive says a name stands in a text, it prints the line and names the
volume and the line number, and the reader can go to the book and see for himself.

**The Arda Archive is not affiliated with, endorsed by, approved by, or connected to
The Tolkien Estate Limited, the Tolkien Trust, HarperCollins, Middle-earth Enterprises, or
any other rights holder.** No part of this site should be read as suggesting otherwise.

## What is quoted

The archive holds a register of **260 works, 14,326,937 words**, graded across four tiers —
Tolkien himself and Christopher Tolkien's editions first, then works published with the
Estate's approval, then scholarship and reference, then material outside the canon.

Of those 260 works, **140 are quoted on the published pages.**

- **653 pages** are published. **593 of them print at least one quotation.**
- **4,335 quotations** are printed in total, of which **3,888 are distinct** — a line
  bearing on two subjects is printed on both pages.
- The distinct quotations come to **52,566 words**, against **14,326,937** words in the
  registered corpus: **0.37 %.**
- **4,165 citations** on the pages resolve to a specific volume and line number.
  By tier: **2,925** into Tolkien's and Christopher Tolkien's own texts, **271** into
  Estate-approved works, **951** into scholarship, **18** into material outside the canon.

## At what length

This is the number that matters most, so here it is without rounding.

| | characters | words |
|---|---|---|
| shortest | 3 | 1 |
| **median** | **72** | **13** |
| 90th percentile | 103 | 18 |
| 95th percentile | 126 | 20 |
| 99th percentile | 220 | 41 |
| longest | 1,443 | 287 |

**A typical quotation on this site is one sentence — thirteen words.** The generator that
builds a record page takes a corpus line as the matcher found it, and a matched line is
about that long.

**And there is a tail, which it would be dishonest to leave out.** **83** quotations run
past 200 characters and **19** past 400. The longest is a 287-word passage on
`person/arwen.html`. These sit on a small number of pages where a single connected passage
is the evidence, and cutting it would misrepresent it. They are not the norm, and this
page is not going to claim they do not exist.

A hard cap applies to the record pages: **no more than eight corpus lines per record**,
which at the measured median is about a third of a printed leaf.

## Whether the quotations carry their source

Nearly always, and here is the exact figure rather than a claim.

- In the archive's stored apparatus — **7,742 quotations across 582 records** —
  **100 %** carry the volume, the line number, and the marker saying what kind of witness
  the text is.
- On the pages as a reader sees them, **97.7 %** of quoted passages sit in a block that
  carries a citation. Most of the remainder are quoted words inside the archive's own
  prose — a term of art in quotation marks — not corpus quotations at all. The errata
  pages carry their sources as a trailing attribution line rather than a footnote, which
  is why an automatic count undercounts them.
- **Three genuine corpus quotations are currently printed with no source named**, on
  `404.html`, `oaths.html`, and the layout prototype. They are hand-written pages rather
  than generated ones. **That is a fault by this archive's own standard and it is recorded
  here rather than tidied away.**

## What the archive quotes and what it will not reproduce

The distinction the archive draws is between **quoting a text so a claim can be checked**
and **reproducing a work so it can be enjoyed in place of the original.** It tries hard to
stay on the first side of that line.

- **The corpus is never published.** The extracted text of the books is held only to
  measure and quote from; it is excluded from every public repository, and always has
  been.
- **Tolkien's own drawings are not reproduced.** The archive's heraldry register holds
  **33 heraldic devices drawn by J.R.R. Tolkien**, every one marked *"© the Tolkien
  Estate — no licence is held"* and every one marked not publishable. **None is on this
  site.** They are described from; they are not shown.
- **Pauline Baynes' 1969 poster map is not shown.** The archive traced its centrelines to
  place names, and publishes Tolkien's notes from it on the authority of the books that
  print them. Her hand-lettering is not traced and the sheet is not reproduced.
- **Publisher matter is filtered out.** Front matter, copyright notices, ISBNs and index
  fragments are lines a corpus file contains but the author did not write, and a filter
  removes them before anything is quoted. **This filter has failed twice**, most recently
  on 26 August 2026, when the Estate's trademark notice — spelled *"trade marks"*, which
  the filter's pattern missed — stood on two pages set as though it were Tolkien's prose.
  It was removed the same day and the filter widened. It is named here because a repair
  nobody mentions is a repair everybody stops checking.

## The rights

**The rights in Tolkien's text belong to their holders, not to this archive.** So do the
rights in Christopher Tolkien's editorial work, and in the work of every other author,
editor and publisher whose books the archive cites. The names, the invented scripts and
languages, and the marks associated with them are likewise the subject of claims by their
holders.

Nothing on this site grants anyone any right in any of it, and nothing about the archive's
own licence (`LICENCE.md`, beside this file) changes that. **Quoted text does not become
reusable by passing through this archive.**

## If you would rather we did not

If you hold rights in something quoted or shown here, or you believe a quotation is too
long, wrongly attributed, or should not be here at all:

**Open an issue at <https://github.com/samsikallo/arda-archive/issues>.**

Say which page and which passage. You do not need to explain yourself at length and you
will not be argued with.

**Removal is genuinely easy here, and that is by design.** The site is generated: every
page is rebuilt from data by code, so a quotation, a plate or a whole volume of sources
can be withdrawn and the archive regenerated without it, usually the same day. The archive
would rather take something down and turn out to have been entitled to keep it than keep
something and turn out not to have been.
