Fingolfin, High King of the Noldor

A symmetric triptych on worn parchment with one punch-hole at the top left: zoomorphic interlace panels of beasts and birds to left and right, and an arched niche between them holding a mail-clad warrior with sword and shield. The heaviest ochre of the eleven. No gutter fold (measured trough depth 11.5).

What the corpus attests

TITLE ATTESTED, FIGURE INVENTED. Fingolfin is High King of the Noldor in Middle-earth -- Silmarillion ch. 13. The corpus gives no portrait, and the mail, the nasal helm and the spiral-charged kite shield in this plate are the plate's own invention, not a device Tolkien blazoned.

Why this volume

Volume II is Annals, Wars and Reckonings -- 'when things happened'. Fingolfin led the host of the Noldor over the Helcaraxe and held the Siege of Angband for nearly four hundred years; the annals of Beleriand are largely the annals of his kingship.

The last stand of Turgon

A crowned king with a blazing white sword amid a press of warriors, haloed in gold, inside a rich interlace border with a large spiral at each corner. The most saturated of the eleven.

What the corpus attests

EVENT ATTESTED, COMPOSITION INVENTED. Gondolin falls and Turgon dies in the ruin of his tower -- Silmarillion ch. 23. The press of warriors, the blazing sword and the gold halo are the plate's composition; the texts describe none of them.

Why this volume

Volume II is Annals, Wars and Reckonings. The Fall of Gondolin is the last battle of the Elder Days that the annals record in full, and Turgon's death in the ruin of his own tower closes the account of the Noldorin kingdoms.

Fingolfin before the gates of Angband

A portrait leaf on an aged cream ground with a soft deckle. Fingolfin in violet and mail strikes upward with a sword and round shield at a huge black crowned figure wreathed in flame swinging a great mace. A broad interlace border with spirals at the corners, blue, gold and red.

What the corpus attests

SCENE FIRMLY ATTESTED -- the best-attested of the eleven. Fingolfin rides alone to Angband and challenges Morgoth; Morgoth comes forth with Grond, and Fingolfin wounds him seven times before he is crushed -- Silmarillion ch. 18, Of the Ruin of Beleriand. Ringil, Grond, the seven wounds and Morgoth's permanent limp are all in the text. The faces are still invention; the EVENT is not.

Why this volume

Volume II is Annals, Wars and Reckonings. This is the single combat the annals of Beleriand turn on, and it is the ONE PLATE OF THE ELEVEN whose scene the corpus describes in detail -- which is why it is placed in the volume of the record rather than the volume of persons.
