etymology

  • The name Thranduil means "Vigorous spring" in Sindarin, from tharan ("vigorous") and lenited tuil ("spring"). Though the name is said to be of Silvan origin, Tolkien's notes on tharan state it was used only in Sindarin.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, "Words, Phrases and Passages in Various Tongues in The Lord of the Rings", in Parma Eldalamberon XVII (edited by Christopher Gilson), pp. 27, 187

history

  • Thranduil's birth date and early life are not recorded. He may have been born in the First Age, or early Second Age. He was of Sindarin descent. His father Oropher was originally one of the Iathrim of Doriath.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Unfinished Tales, "The History of Galadriel and Celeborn", "Appendix B: The Sindarin Princes of the Silvan Elves"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Thranduil”, revid 432422 (2026-01-29T12:28:12Z)

  • Many of the Sindar left Lindon and travelled eastward before the building of the Barad-dûr in . Thranduil is first recorded in this event, when he and his father, Oropher, arrived "with only a handful of Sindar" in , in Greenwood the Great. Oropher was taken by the Silvan Elves as their lord and founded the Woodland Realm.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix B: The Tale of Years (Chronology of the Westlands); J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Unfinished Tales, "The History of Galadriel and Celeborn", "Appendix B: The Sindarin Princes of the Silvan Elves"; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Peoples of Middle-earth, "VI. The Tale of Years of the Second Age"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Thranduil”, revid 432422 (2026-01-29T12:28:12Z)

  • The Elves of Mirkwood joined the Last Alliance and in , Thranduil followed his father and numerous lightly armed Elves in the War of the Last Alliance. In the Battle of Dagorlad, Oropher was slain in the first assault upon Mordor, "rushing forward at the head of his most doughty warriors before Gil-galad had given the signal for the advance." His son survived, but over the course of the war, two-thirds of his people had perished. After the Siege of Barad-dûr in , when Sauron was defeated, Thranduil led the remainder of his people north back to the Woodland Realm, where he was crowned king.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Unfinished Tales, "The History of Galadriel and Celeborn", "Appendix B: The Sindarin Princes of the Silvan Elves"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Thranduil”, revid 432422 (2026-01-29T12:28:12Z)

  • The partition of Eryn Lasgalen is the last reference to Thranduil in the histories of Middle-earth. It is not said that he joined his son Legolas in settling Ithilien or in departing over the Sea. Presumably, he continued to rule the re-established Woodland Realm into the Fourth Age, but nothing of this is known for certain, as he could have sailed to the West at some unknown date.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix B, "Later Events Concerning the Members of the Fellowship of the Ring"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Thranduil”, revid 432422 (2026-01-29T12:28:12Z)

  • After the Dwarves' escape, Thranduil received word of what had transpired from the Raft-elves who returned up the Forest River. Thus he was now aware of the Dwarves' quest. Upon learning this, he stated, "no treasure will come back through Mirkwood without my having something to say in the matter." He believed the Dwarves were incapable of slaying the Dragon. However, he soon heard from his own messengers (including the birds) that the Dragon Smaug had been felled (and the Lake-town was destroyed).

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Hobbit, "A Warm Welcome"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Thranduil”, revid 432422 (2026-01-29T12:28:12Z)

  • Thranduil was aware that Smaug had a massive hoard of treasure. Believing the Dwarves to be dead, and desiring a share of the treasure, he set out towards the Lonely Mountain with a company of Elves. On the way, they met messengers from Bard who was seeking aid for his destroyed town. Thranduil gave aid to these Men, as the Lake-men had been friends with the Wood-elves, and left elven craftsmen to aid in the labour and building of huts to fortify them against the coming winter. Together, Thranduil and Bard led their forces towards the Lonely Mountain and were surprised when they found out that the Dwarves not only survived Smaug's attacks, but had taken possession of the Mountain and its treasures; the King under the Mountain had returned. thumb|left|The Arkenstone by [[Ted Nasmith]]

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Hobbit, "Fire and Water"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Thranduil”, revid 432422 (2026-01-29T12:28:12Z)

  • Upon the return of Sauron - disguised as the Necromancer - in around , southern Greenwood became increasingly dangerous; evil creatures such as the great spiders came to dwell in it and the forest soon came to be known as "Mirkwood". Thranduil's folk retreated to the north of the forest, where they established themselves near the Forest River. Thranduil had his halls delved underground, fashioned partly in memory (it was said) of the mighty, but long-lost, Menegroth of Doriath. In north-eastern Mirkwood, they were near the Forest River behind great, stone gates.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix B, "The Third Age"; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Unfinished Tales, "The History of Galadriel and Celeborn", "Appendix B: The Sindarin Princes of the Silvan Elves"; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Two Towers, "The Road to Isengard"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Thranduil”, revid 432422 (2026-01-29T12:28:12Z)

  • On 21 March, Aragorn and Gandalf delivered Gollum as a prisoner to Thranduil; but in June he later escaped. Thranduil sent his son Legolas to Rivendell to inform Elrond, and during the Council of Elrond Legolas was selected as one of the nine members of the Fellowship of the Ring. Another member of the Company was the Dwarf, Gimli, the son of Glóin of Thorin's company, who had previously been imprisoned by Thranduil. The unprecedented friendship between Elf and Dwarf helped to reconcile Thranduil's people and the Dwarves.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "The Council of Elrond"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Thranduil”, revid 432422 (2026-01-29T12:28:12Z)

  • On 15 March, , Sauron's army from Dol Guldur attacked Mirkwood. There was a long battle under the trees and the woods were set on fire, but in the end Thranduil was victorious. And after the passing of Sauron, the forces of the Lord and Lady of Galadhrim stormed Dol Guldur and threw down its walls, finally cleansing the forest.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix B, "The Great Years"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Thranduil”, revid 432422 (2026-01-29T12:28:12Z)

  • On 6 April (the day of the Elven New Year), the Elvenking met with Celeborn in the midst of Mirkwood and renamed it Eryn Lasgalen, "The Wood of the Greenleaves". With the forest cleansed and Sauron's forces destroyed, the forest was divided; Celeborn claimed the southern part of the forest north to the Narrows, Thranduil took the northern part of the forest south to the Mountains, and the two Elf-lords granted the middle to the Beornings and Woodmen.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix B, "The Chief Days from the Fall of Barad-dûr to the End of the Third Age"; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix B, "The Great Years"

other versions

  • The published Appendix B of The Lord of the Rings, as well as one of its drafts, mention Thranduil as King of the Woodland realm, founded in , suggesting (but not explicitly mentioning) that he was possibly its founder and first king. His father Oropher was invented later, while Tolkien was fleshing out the background of Thranduil and the history of the Silvan realms, given a far older origin, explicitly clarifying that Thranduil was not the founder of the realm, and he only succeeded his father after the War of the Last Alliance. Oropher and Thranduil's backstory were never incorporated in more "canonical" narratives, and the drafts exploring them were published in the Unfinished Tales.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Peoples of Middle-earth, "VI. The Tale of Years of the Second Age"; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Unfinished Tales, "The History of Galadriel and Celeborn", "Appendix B: The Sindarin Princes of the Silvan Elves"
