history

  • In , the alliance known as Union of Maedhros led Elves, Dwarves, and Men to assault Angband. Húrin led his folk to join the host led by the High King Fingon. In the battle, later named the Nirnaeth Arnoediad or Battle of Unnumbered Tears, the Union were defeated by the hosts of Morgoth. Many Elves and Men perished, including Fingon who was slain by a Balrog. With their liege lord slain and facing a rout, Húrin and Huor convinced Turgon to withdraw and keep the secret of Gondolin. To defend his escape, the Men of Dor-lómin formed a rearguard near the Pass of Sirion and held it giving as much time for Turgon to gather the remainder of Fingon's people and the host of Gondolin to escape. The location of Gondolin remained secret but for the Men of the Dor-lómin this was their last stand for they would not forsake the Northlands and were slaughtered by the hosts of Morgoth. By the Fen of Serech, Huor was slain by an arrow through the eye, but Húrin remained steadfast crying Aurë entuluva!(S) for each foe he slew with his axe, seventy times in all. He fought until he was buried under the weight of bodies of his enemies, then Gothmog bound him and dragged him towards Angband.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Fifth Battle: Nirnaeth Arnoediad"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Húrin”, revid 441638 (2026-08-22T23:44:36Z)

  • After the death of his children Morgoth released Húrin, where he hoped he could further his malice. The broken man first returned to Hithlum, which was now ruled by Easterlings. Finding none of his kin, he travelled with outlaws to the mountains of Echoriath, searching for the entrance to Gondolin. At first Turgon refused him, but later recanted and sent Eagles to bring him to Gondolin. Húrin had already left for the Forest of Brethil, but Morgoth's curse had already ensnared Gondolin, for the Dark Lord's spies now knew the general location of the Hidden Kingdom.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Ruin of Doriath"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Húrin”, revid 441638 (2026-08-22T23:44:36Z)

  • Húrin continued on to the ruins of Nargothrond; beyond seeking for news and plunder, he had been an admirer of Finrod. In the ruins he found the Petty-dwarf Mîm. The petty-dwarf had betrayed Túrin years ago and in revenge Húrin slaughtered him. Mîm had claimed the treasure of Nargothrond, but Húrin recovered the Nauglamír and journeyed eastwards.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The War of the Jewels, "Part Three. The Wanderings of Húrin and Other Writings not forming part of the Quenta Silmarillion: I. The Wanderings of Húrin"; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Ruin of Doriath"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Húrin”, revid 441638 (2026-08-22T23:44:36Z)

  • Húrin reached the Meres of Twilight and was taken by the Elves who guarded the western borders of Doriath in . He was allowed to enter Menegroth, where in anger he threw the Nauglamír before King Thingol and 'thanked' him for aiding his son. Thingol's wife, Melian, finally pierced through Húrin's madness and grief revealing the truth that Morgoth hid. Shamed by his actions, he picked up the Nauglamír to give to the king as a gift and memorial, then he left Menegroth no longer a thrall of Morgoth.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The War of the Jewels, "Part Three. The Wanderings of Húrin and Other Writings not forming part of the Quenta Silmarillion: I. The Wanderings of Húrin", p. 258; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Ruin of Doriath"

other versions

  • In The Book of Lost Tales, Úrin the Steadfast was a lord of Men who resided in the woodlands. He had a wife, Mavwin and a son, Túrin. Different from many of his folk, he had been a friend of the Elves, most especially the Gnome Egnor, an Elf of the greenwood and renown huntsman. He also knew Beren, son of Egnor, and had once rendered him service in respect of Damrod, son of Beren. He was said to have "marched with countless battalions" alongside Egnor, and been as a brother in arms to Egnor which was emended to him being a brother in arms to Beren.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Book of Lost Tales Part Two, "II. Turambar and the Foalókë"; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Book of Lost Tales Part One, "X. Gilfanon's Tale: The Travail of the Noldoli and the Coming of Mankind"; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Book of Lost Tales Part Two, "I. The Tale of Tinúviel": "The second version of the Tale of Tinúviel"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Húrin”, revid 441638 (2026-08-22T23:44:36Z)

  • Úrin hearkened to the summons of the Gnomes and with his folk marched with the Ilkorindi against Melko. In the ensuing Battle of Lamentation the people of Úrin were among the few kindreds of Men who did not flee from battle, and were instead slain except for Úrin who was made captive.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Book of Lost Tales Part Two, "II. Turambar and the Foalókë"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Húrin”, revid 441638 (2026-08-22T23:44:36Z)

  • Melko then attempted to constrain Úrin to take up his employ, and seek after Turgon as a spy. However, neither threats of torture nor promises of riches would cause Úrin to yield. In anger, Melko then cursed a doom of woe upon Úrin and his folk, magically bound Úrin to a lofty place in the mountains, and gave Úrin a measure of vision by which he might see those things which befell his wife and children and be powerless to aid. After Melko gloated that “the life of Túrin thy son shall be accounted a matter for tears wherever Elves or Men are gathered for the telling of tales”, Úrin replied that “At least none shall pity him for this, that he had a craven for father.” Shortly after, Mavwin gave birth to their daughter, Nienóri, and sent Túrin to foster with Tinwelint. Due to the renown of Úrin, and his bond with Beren, Tinwelint accepted Túrin not as a retainer, but as even a second child.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Book of Lost Tales Part Two, "II. Turambar and the Foalókë"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Húrin”, revid 441638 (2026-08-22T23:44:36Z)

  • As the years passed, the dread happenings of Mavwin, Túrin, and Nienóri were witnessed by Úrin, and though Melko continually tempted him to yield, Úrin refused. However, after the deaths of Túrin, and Nienóri, the doom of Úrin's folk had been utterly fulfilled and Melko thought to use him in a more subtle way. Melko put evil words of distrust to Úrin, and then released him.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Book of Lost Tales Part Two, "II. Turambar and the Foalókë"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Húrin”, revid 441638 (2026-08-22T23:44:36Z)

  • Úrin, bowed with years and sorrow, and bitter in spirit, then departed unmolested from Melko's lands. Around himself he gathered a band of exiled wild Elves who were fierce and lawless. These he led to the caves of the Rodothlim where they encountered Mîm the Dwarf and the cursed gold. Though Mîm warned them not to touch the treasure, Úrin's outlaws made to carry it off regardless. When Mîm then began cursing them for doing so, Úrin smote the Dwarf, and Mîm then lay one final curse upon the gold before his death.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Book of Lost Tales Part Two, "II. Turambar and the Foalókë"

  • Úrin then ordered his outlaws to carry the gold to the halls of Tinwelint. They murmured against doing so, but Úrin responded that Tinwelint, that "king of greed", would reward them with a much sweeter life for the treasure than all the gold in Valinor could get them out in the empty woods. The hoard was then arranged for transport, placed in sacks or shut in boxes of rough wood, and so great was it that Úrin's company could scarce bear it. Úrin eventually brought it to Tinwelint's hall, and there he was greeted with joy and amazement. Úrin rebuked this welcome though, and fiercely told Tinwelint and his court of the unfortunate fates of Túrin and Nienóri. He then revealed the vast treasure, mockingly gifted it to Tinwelint as payment in full his services, and insulted him. At this Tinwelint lost his patience, and chastised Úrin and ordered him to be gone. He would not, however, have Úrin slain out of remembrance for his greet deeds of valiance.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Book of Lost Tales Part Two, "II. Turambar and the Foalókë"

The Elves of Kôr], however, further tell that Úrin and Mavwin eventually fared to Mandos, and there they prayed to Manwë so that the Gods would have mercy on the unhappy fate of Túrin and Ninóri.
