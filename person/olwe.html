history

  • Olwë and his people dwelt on the island of Tol Eressëa for a while, where the Teleri could be by the sea whilst also bathing in the light of the Two Trees. These people became the Falmari. When they finally came to Eldamar some centuries later, the Falmari, under the lordship of Olwë, began the building of Alqualondë with the aid of the Noldor.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Morgoth's Ring, "Part Two. The Annals of Aman": &sect;72; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Coming of the Elves and the Captivity of Melkor"; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Morgoth's Ring, "Part Two. The Annals of Aman"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Olwë”, revid 435228 (2026-02-19T18:24:10Z)

  • When Fëanor revolted against the Valar, he attempted to persuade the Teleri to join him in order to obtain their ships. They were unmoved, wanting no other realm than Eldamar and no other lord than Olwë. Fëanor reproached Olwë and his people as "fainthearted loiterers" and reminded them that the Noldor had aided them when they first arrived. Olwë rebuked Fëanor's folly and refused to give him any ships of the Teleri, which they valued as the Noldor did their gems. Fëanor withdrew but then led his forces to Alqualondë to take the ships by force, leading to the first Kinslaying. As the Noldor rowed the stolen vessels northward, Olwë called for Ossë to stop them, but it was not permitted by the Valar.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Flight of the Noldor"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Olwë”, revid 435228 (2026-02-19T18:24:10Z)

  • His daughter Eärwen married Finarfin son of Finwë and later king of the remaining Noldor. Besides Eärwen, Olwë had several sons.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of Eldamar and the Princes of the Eldalië"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Olwë”, revid 435228 (2026-02-19T18:24:10Z)

  • Olwë was born in Cuiviénen and was the younger brother of Elwë, one of the ambassadors taken by Oromë to Valinor to see the Two Trees. When the ambassadors returned, they urged their peoples to hear the summons of Oromë and go to the West. Because their clan was the largest, it was divided in two hosts under the two brothers and together, they led their people.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Coming of the Elves and the Captivity of Melkor"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Olwë”, revid 435228 (2026-02-19T18:24:10Z)

  • The Nelyar were the most unwilling during the Great Journey and Olwë's host was always the hindmost. Some of Olwë's host were terrified by the shadow of the Misty Mountains and preferred to settle by the woodlands of the Vales of Anduin.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Coming of the Elves and the Captivity of Melkor"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Olwë”, revid 435228 (2026-02-19T18:24:10Z)

  • During a long wait in Beleriand, Elwë disappeared. After years of searching, Olwë grew impatient, and led the greater part of the Teleri to Valinor, on the insistence of the Vala Ulmo.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of Thingol and Melian"; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of Eldamar and the Princes of the Eldalië"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Olwë”, revid 435228 (2026-02-19T18:24:10Z)

other names

  • In one manuscript, the Telerin form of Olwë is said to be Olue. In another manuscript the Telerin form is similar to the Quenya form: Olwë. The Falmari sometimes called him Volwë. Another one of his names was Ciriáran (meaning "mariner king").

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The War of the Jewels, "Part Four. Quendi and Eldar: A. The principal linguistic elements concerned", p. 369; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Peoples of Middle-earth, "XI. The Shibboleth of Fëanor", "Notes", Note 20, p. 357; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Peoples of Middle-earth, "XI. The Shibboleth of Fëanor", "The names of Finwë's descendants" p. 341

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Olwë”, revid 435228 (2026-02-19T18:24:10Z)

other versions

  • In the earlier versions of the Quenta Silmarillion, Olwë was named Elwë (while his brother Thingol also had the name Sindo), and there it was Olwë who was one of the Three Ambassadors instead of his brother Thingol. Neither did the existence of his daughter Eärwen or her marriage to Finarfin emerge yet.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Lost Road and Other Writings, "Part Two: Valinor and Middle-earth before The Lord of the Rings, V. The Lhammas", Of the tongues of the Elves in Middle-earth, and of the Noldorin that returned thither, p. 174; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Lost Road and Other Writings, "Part Two: Valinor and Middle-earth before The Lord of the Rings, VI. Quenta Silmarillion", Commentary on Chapter 3(a), p. 217
