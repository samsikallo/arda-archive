etymology

  • The name Meril is the Sindarin word for rose used as a name.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Sauron Defeated, "Part One: The End of the Third Age: XI. The Epilogue", pp. 129-31

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Meril (mother of Gil-galad)”, revid 419487 (2025-03-08T07:23:31Z)

other versions

  • An otherwise unnamed wife of Finrod appears yet again (though not in any explicit association with Gil-galad) in a note from the late 1960s

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Peoples of Middle-earth, "X. Of Dwarves and Men", "Notes", note 7, pp. 317-8

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Meril (mother of Gil-galad)”, revid 419487 (2025-03-08T07:23:31Z)

  • Felagund's wife appears again (this time without a name) in a pencilled note near the opening of the Beren and Lúthien chapter of the Quenta Silmarillion

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The War of the Jewels, "Part Two. The Later Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Ruin of Beleriand and the Fall of Fingolfin (Chapter 15)", p. 242

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Meril (mother of Gil-galad)”, revid 419487 (2025-03-08T07:23:31Z)

  • However, this note was struck out, and somewhat further in the Beren and Lúthien chapter (in the part referring to Orodreth's expulsion of Celegorm and Curufin) there appears another note wherein the unnamed wife of Inglor "forsook the folk of Nargothrond and went with her son Gilgalad to the Havens of the Falas".

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The War of the Jewels, "Part Two. The Later Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Ruin of Beleriand and the Fall of Fingolfin (Chapter 15)", p. 242

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Meril (mother of Gil-galad)”, revid 419487 (2025-03-08T07:23:31Z)

  • This has Felagund's wife and son (Gil-galad) only leaving Nargothrond after the expulsion of Celegorm and Curufin (in ) or later.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The War of the Jewels, "Part One. The Grey Annals": &sect;182
