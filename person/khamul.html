history

  • After the Witch-king, Khamûl had the best ability to perceive the presence of the One Ring, but his power was most confused and diminished by daylight.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Unfinished Tales, "The Hunt for the Ring", Notes, note 1

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Khamûl”, revid 440650 (2026-08-01T20:10:12Z)

  • From 20 March on, Khamûl and the other seven remaining Ringwraiths were flying on their Fell beasts over the Army of the West watching its movements as it marched to the Black Gate.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix B, "The Great Years", p. 1094; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Return of the King, "The Black Gate Opens","near the end of the second day of their march from the Cross-roads", p. 885

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Khamûl”, revid 440650 (2026-08-01T20:10:12Z)

  • On 25 March, Khamûl and all the other seven Ringwraiths were flying on their Fell beasts over the Army of the West during the Battle of the Morannon. When Frodo put on the ring at the Cracks of Doom in Mount Doom Sauron realised the danger and ordered Khâmul and the other Ringwraiths to race to Mount Doom. Khamûl probably perished with the rest of the Ringwraiths when they flew on their Fell beasts to Mount Doom and were caught in the fiery eruption of Mount Doom that took place after the destruction of the One Ring, because they "crackled, withered, and went out".

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix B, "The Great Years", p. 1094; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Return of the King, "The Black Gate Opens", p. 887; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Return of the King, "Mount Doom", p. 946-7

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Khamûl”, revid 440650 (2026-08-01T20:10:12Z)

  • In , Sauron declared himself openly to his enemies and sent Khamûl and two other Ringwraiths to Dol Guldur, with the former taking command.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix B, "The Third Age", entry for the year 2951, p. 1089; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Unfinished Tales, "The Hunt for the Ring", "(i) Of the Journey of the Black Riders"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Khamûl”, revid 440650 (2026-08-01T20:10:12Z)

  • Around 22 July , Khamûl with another Ringwraith who lived at Dol Guldur met the Witch-king and six other Ringwraiths that lived at Minas Morgul in the Field of Celebrant. The Ringwraiths from Dol Guldur informed their fellow Ringwraiths from Minas Morgul that Gollum had escaped and vanished. Khamûl also told them that the villages of the Stoors at the river Gladden had been deserted for a long time and that it had not been possible to find a dwelling of Halflings in the Vales of Anduin.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Unfinished Tales, "The Hunt for the Ring", "(i) Of the Journey of the Black Riders"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Khamûl”, revid 440650 (2026-08-01T20:10:12Z)

  • In the evening of 23 September, Khamûl arrived in Hobbiton and asked Hamfast Gamgee about "Baggins". Hamfast Gamgee misled him that Frodo Baggins had already left and he rode to the east. On 24 September, he rode on the Stock Road and overtook Frodo and stopped, because he sensed the Ring, but he was not certain because of the bright daylight and left again and waited until the night. After the sunset he sensed the ring again and pursued Frodo. He did not dare to attack and retreated, because he was intimidated by the Elves and by the song about Elbereth. He could not sense the ring clearly as long as Frodo was surrounded by the Elves.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix B, "The Great Years", pp. 1091-2; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "Three is Company", p. 74; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "Three is Company", p. 78

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Khamûl”, revid 440650 (2026-08-01T20:10:12Z)

  • After the departure of the Elves Khamûl continued to pursue Frodo, but did not find him and called the other Ringwraith from Dol Guldur to him with cries. He and another Ringwraith from Dol Guldur went east over the fields and Khamûl visited Farmer Maggot. After leaving farmer Maggot he sent his companion off to take the Causeway in the direction of the Overbourn Marshes and he went north in the direction of the Brandywine Bridge. He arrived too late at the Bucklebury Ferry in the night of 25 September.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "A Short Cut to Mushrooms", pp. 93-4

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Khamûl”, revid 440650 (2026-08-01T20:10:12Z)

  • He was also one of the six Ringwraiths, led by the Witch King, that followed Gandalf on Shadowfax and attacked him on 3 October during the night on Weathertop, but were repulsed after a violent fight in the early hours of 4 October, when Frodo and Aragorn saw the lights of that battle from their camp. The Witch King and Khamûl stayed behind to watch Weathertop (waiting for the Ring Bearer to come) for two days thereafter, along with two other Ringwraiths and sent four Ringwraiths to pursue Gandalf.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix B, "The Great Years", pp. 1091-2; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "A Knife in the Dark", p. 183

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Khamûl”, revid 440650 (2026-08-01T20:10:12Z)

  • Khamûl and two other Ringwraiths were on the bridge over the Hoarwell on 11 October, but when Glorfindel approached them, they withdrew and were pursued by Glorfindel down the road until they left the road and scattered. However on 14 October, Khamûl, the Witch-king and three other Ringwraiths assembled again and continued with their pursuit.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix B, "The Great Years", pp. 1091-2; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "Flight to the Ford", p. 210

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Khamûl”, revid 440650 (2026-08-01T20:10:12Z)

  • On 20 October, he was among the Ringwraiths who caught up with Frodo and his companions and dared to use the ford to ride into the river Loudwater to pursue Frodo who had already crossed the river, but they were swept down the river by the flood that was called by Elrond.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix B, "The Great Years", pp. 1091-2; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "Flight to the Ford", pp. 212-5; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "Many Meetings", p. 224

other versions

  • The bynames "the Second Chief (the Black Easterling)" were used for an unnamed Ringwraith that was based in Dol Guldur in a rejected version of the passage about the earlier movements of the Ringwraiths in "The Hunt for the Ring", but not in the final version where the Ringwraith who was Sauron's lieutenant at Dol Guldur was named Khâmul the Shadow of the East.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Unfinished Tales, "The Hunt for the Ring", Notes, note 1
