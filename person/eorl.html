etymology

  • The word Eorl is an Old English translation of genuine Rohanese þuron, which has perhaps influence from Elvish root TUR. Eorl is an Old English word referring to a person of noble rank. It is the ancestor of modern "earl". It was the highest rank of nobility in North Germanic culture, not introduced to Britain until the late 9th century, when it replaced the equivalent Old English title ealdorman. The Old Norse equivalent is jarl. Þórsteinn Thorarensen used Jarl as the name for the character throughout his Icelandic translation of The Lord of the Rings, along with Hjálmur, for Helm, Þengill for Thengel, Þjódan for Théoden, and Jómar for Éomer. Compare Ceorl, the second rank.
