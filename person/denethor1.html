etymology

  • For the meaning of his name refer to Denethor - Etymology. It is possible that Denethor I was named after the Nando chieftain of the Laiquendi of the First Age.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Carl F. Hostetter (ed.), "Part Two. Body, Mind and Spirit: V. Beards", The Nature of Middle-earth, HarperCollins, 2021, footnote 3, p. 189

history

  • Denethor was probably named after the leader of the Laiquendi in the First Age, as it was customary in the House of Húrin to name sons after figures from the legends of the Noldor and their interactions with the Edain.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Peoples of Middle-earth, "VII. The Heirs of Elendil", manuscript C, The Ruling Stewards of Gondor, p. 203

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Denethor I”, revid 422415 (2025-07-28T14:25:01Z)

  • During the time of Denethor’s rule the Watchful Peace came to an end (in ) when Sauron returned with increased strength to Dol Guldur. In the last years of his rule the Uruks, black Orcs with great strength, came from Mordor for the first time and assailed and overran Ithilien and captured Osgiliath in . Denethor’s son, Boromir, defeated the invaders and reconquered Ithilien, but Osigiliath was left in ruins and its great stone-bridge over the river Anduin was broken and Osgiliath was abandoned by its population.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix B, "The Third Age", entry for the year 2460, p. 1087; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix A, "The Númenorean Kings", "Gondor and the Heirs of Anárion", "The Stewards", entry for Steward Denethor I, p. 1053

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Denethor I”, revid 422415 (2025-07-28T14:25:01Z)

  • The Rammas Echor was built with a great amount of labour after the province of Ithilien fell under the shadow of the Enemy during the rule of Steward Denethor, but it is not specified if it was during the rule of Steward Denethor I or Steward Denethor II. It is possible that Denethor I had the Rammas Echor constructed after the capture of Osgiliath, to protect the Pelennor Fields which fed Minas Tirith, because the borders of Gondor were under constant threat from his rule on.
