etymology

  • Felagund was an epessë given to him by the Dwarves that expanded the caves of Nargothrond, and meant "Hewer of Caves". It is not Sindarin, but rather Sindarinized Khuzdul, from Felakgundu. The name was Eldarized by other as Felagon (in relation with Fingon, Turgon).

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Peoples of Middle-earth, "XI. The Shibboleth of Fëanor", "The Dwarvish origin of the name Felagund", p. 352

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Finrod”, revid 439346 (2026-06-15T10:59:54Z)

  • While revising his Elvish languages in later years, Tolkien reinterpreted Felagund as "den-dweller, brock, badger", from Sindarin fela, which was used for dens made by wild animals or temporary dwellings of wandering folks, unrelated to big carved caves. The west bank of Narog had many dens of badgers, some of them occupied by Petty-dwarves. It was thus probable that Felagund was actually given by the Sons of Fëanor as a mockery to Finrod, and transmitted by the Dwarves.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Carl F. Hostetter (ed.), "Part Three. The World, its Lands, and its Inhabitants: VII. The Founding of Nargothrond", The Nature of Middle-earth, HarperCollins, 2021, p. 304

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Finrod”, revid 439346 (2026-06-15T10:59:54Z)

  • An early etymology for Felagund was "Lord of Caves" in Noldorin, from fela ("caverns") + cunn ("prince").

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Lost Road and Other Writings, Part Three: "The Etymologies", entries"KUNDU", "PHELEG"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Finrod”, revid 439346 (2026-06-15T10:59:54Z)

  • The name Finrod is the Sindarin form of his father-name Findaráto ("[Golden-]Haired Champion"). It is possible he and his brother Angrod were first called Aráto, and later differentiated adding find-, referred to the golden hair derived from Indis. This name was Telerin in form where the adjectival element ("aráto" in this case) was typically placed second while in Quenya it would have preceded. Thus, the more natural Quenya form would have been Artafindë.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Peoples of Middle-earth, "XI. The Shibboleth of Fëanor", "The names of Finwë's descendants", p. 346–347

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Finrod”, revid 439346 (2026-06-15T10:59:54Z)

history

  • Finrod participated in the Dagor Bragollach, fighting in the Fen of Serech, when he was surrounded by Orcs. It was Barahir of the House of Bëor who saved his life, and Finrod swore an oath of abiding friendship and aid in every need to Barahir and all his kin. As a token he gave Barahir his ring, which became known as the Ring of Barahir.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Ruin of Beleriand and the Fall of Fingolfin"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Finrod”, revid 439346 (2026-06-15T10:59:54Z)

  • Finrod was born in Eldamar in the Year of the Trees 1300 within the First Age, the same as his cousin Turgon. They were friends, and his beloved was Amarië of the Vanyar.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Morgoth's Ring, "Part Two. The Annals of Aman": Note on &sect;85

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Finrod”, revid 439346 (2026-06-15T10:59:54Z)

  • Finrod was like his father in his fair face and golden hair, and also in noble and generous heart, though he had the high courage of the Noldor and in his youth their eagerness and unrest; and he had also from his Telerin mother a love of the sea and dreams of far lands that he had never seen.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Unfinished Tales, "The History of Galadriel and Celeborn"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Finrod”, revid 439346 (2026-06-15T10:59:54Z)

  • Finrod was among those who opposed Fëanor and the Exile of the Noldor. However, when the Noldor were set to depart from Aman, he joined them, for he would not be sundered from his friends and his people who were eager to go. Also, he had no wish to abandon his people to Fëanor's rule.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Flight of the Noldor"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Finrod”, revid 439346 (2026-06-15T10:59:54Z)

  • The realm of Nargothrond extended to the sea, excluding the Falas. Finrod was a friend and ally of Círdan, and had also built the tower Barad Nimras upon the cape. Unfortunately, the building of Finrod's realm eventually resulted in him being a target of the enmity of the Petty-dwarves. Their chieftain, Mîm, went so far as to attempt to assassinate Finrod while he slept, for which he and his people were expelled from their former territories.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of Beleriand and its Realms"; J.R.R. Tolkien, Carl F. Hostetter (ed.), "Part Three. The World, its Lands, and its Inhabitants: VII. The Founding of Nargothrond", The Nature of Middle-earth, HarperCollins, 2021, p. 305

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Finrod”, revid 439346 (2026-06-15T10:59:54Z)

  • Finrod, along with his father, led the rear of the host along with many of the noblest and wisest of the Noldor; and often they looked behind them to see their fair city, especially Finrod, for he had to leave Amarië behind. With him, he brought along many treasures from Tirion, including his Ring.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Return of the Noldor"; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of Beren and Lúthien"

other names

  • His mother-name was Ingoldo, which was also the mother-name of his father Finarfin. It meant "the Ñoldo", 'one-eminent of the kindred' which is in simpler words 'the wise'. It was a name used by his brothers and sister, who loved him. Finrod was also called Nóm ("Wisdom") by Bëor and his people. His other titles include "the Faithful", "the Friend of Men", "King of Nargothrond", "Lord of Nargothrond". He was often called by the Eldar Edennil or Atandil, which means "Friend of Men" in Sindarin and Quenya respectively. A variant for Atandil was Firindil.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Peoples of Middle-earth, "XI. The Shibboleth of Fëanor", "The names of Finwë's descendants", p. 346, 360; J.R.R. other versions

  • In earlier versions of the Middle-earth myths, and when The Lord of the Rings was published, the name Finrod was given to the character later known as Finarfin. Finrod Felagund was then named Inglor Felagund. However, that changed in later drafts, and the character who had the name Gildor Inglorion, in The Lord of the Rings, was unrelated to Finrod despite having a name that means Gildor son of Inglor. Finrod was childless and unmarried during the events of The Silmarillion because his lover, Amarië, remained in Valinor. In one note, while defining the term vanwa, Tolkien considered that Felagund, like Míriel, chose not to return to life because of a special will of their own. In the published The Silmarillion, Orodreth is Finrod's brother: this was an editorial decision by Christopher Tolkien and an admitted mistake. Orodreth was actually the son of Angrod and thus Finrod's nephew.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Noldor in Beleriand"; J.R.R. Tolkien, "Words, Phrases and Passages in Various Tongues in The Lord of the Rings: Eldarin Roots and Stems", in Parma Eldalamberon XVII (edited by Christopher Gilson), p. 143
