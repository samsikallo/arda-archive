etymology

  • Tarostar is Quenya. Its meaning is not glossed, but as Paul Strack explains, it might mean "King of the Lands" and be a compound of tar ("high, king") and the plural form of sta ("land"). Rómendacil is Quenya. It means "East-victor". It is a compound of rómen ("east") and -(n)dacil ("victor").

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix F, "The Languages and Peoples of the Third Age", "Of Men", footnote, p. 1128; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix A, "The Númenorean Kings", "Gondor and the Heirs of Anárion", entry for Kings Ostoher, Tarostar and Turambar, p. 1044

history

  • In the first invasion of Easterlings fell upon Gondor. Tarostar defeated the invaders in and drove them from Gondor. After his victory against the Easterlings Tarostar assumed the name Rómendacil, "East-victor".

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix B, "The Third Age", entry for the year 490, p. 1085; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix A, "The Númenorean Kings", "Gondor and the Heirs of Anárion", entry for Kings Ostoher, Tarostar and Turambar, p. 1044; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix B, "The Third Age", entry for the year 500, p. 1085

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Rómendacil I”, revid 422401 (2025-07-28T13:05:09Z)

  • After the first assault of the Easterlings, Rómendacil ordered that the "Tradition of Isildur" be set down in a sealed scroll, to be delivered by the Steward of Gondor to the King's heir before his crowning.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Unfinished Tales, "Cirion and Eorl and the Friendship of Gondor and Rohan", "(iv) The Tradition of Isildur"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Rómendacil I”, revid 422401 (2025-07-28T13:05:09Z)

  • Rómendacil's triumph did not end the Easterling threat. In Rómendacil was killed in a battle against new hordes of Easterlings. His son Turambar avenged him and conquered much territory eastwards.
