etymology

  • Andreth is a Sindarin word meaning "long-suffering, 'Patience'". Her other name, Saelind, means "Wise-heart".

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, "Words, Phrases and Passages in Various Tongues in The Lord of the Rings", in Parma Eldalamberon XVII (edited by Christopher Gilson), p. 182; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Morgoth's Ring, "Part Four. Athrabeth Finrod ah Andreth", p. 305

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Andreth”, revid 395785 (2024-06-25T19:06:29Z)

history

  • Andreth was the elder daughter and second child of Boromir and a sister to Bregor and Beril.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The War of the Jewels, "Part Two. The Later Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Coming of Men into the West (Chapter 14)", (i) The House of Bëor, p. 231

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Andreth”, revid 395785 (2024-06-25T19:06:29Z)

  • She had a close friendship with Aegnor's eldest brother, King Finrod of Nargothrond, who often visited her during the Siege to converse with her wisdom on the matters of Elves and Men. One such conversation was written down and later known as Athrabeth Finrod ah Andreth.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Morgoth's Ring, "Part Four. Athrabeth Finrod ah Andreth"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Andreth”, revid 395785 (2024-06-25T19:06:29Z)

other versions

  • In the first drafts of the Athrabeth, Andreth acknowledges she belongs to those of the "Great Hope", while in the final text she remains skeptical about that belief.
