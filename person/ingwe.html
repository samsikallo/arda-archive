etymology

  • The name Ingwë is in Quenya. It means "chief", being derived from the root ING ("highest", "top"), to which is attached the suffix -wë ("person"). The Vanyar called themselves the Ingwer, the chieftains, on account of being considered the leaders and principal kindred of the Eldar, and their king's proper title was Ingwë Ingweron: ‘chief of the chieftains’. In the earlier Etymologies, Ingwë is said to be a compound of ing ("first") + the ending -we ("man").

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Peoples of Middle-earth, "XI. The Shibboleth of Fëanor", "The names of Finwë's descendants", p. 340; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Lost Road and Other Writings, Part Three: "The Etymologies", entries"ING", "WEG"

history

  • Ingwë was one of the Minyar born at Cuiviénen. He was the eldest son of Ilion, and a descendant of Imin and Iminyë, from eldest son to eldest son. He was beloved by his people, and at some point before the Great Journey he wed Ilwen and had several children at Cuiviénen by the time Oromë found the Quendi.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Carl F. Hostetter (ed.), "Part One. Time and Ageing: XVII. Generational Schemes", The Nature of Middle-earth, HarperCollins, 2021, pp. 127-128

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Ingwë”, revid 421819 (2025-07-07T10:53:35Z)

  • When Oromë invited them to Aman, Ingwë, along with Finwë and Elwë, were the most eager to visit, and so followed him as ambassadors and travelled to the Blessed Realm. Once there, Ingwë desired to "dwell in the presence of Varda", and was reluctant to return to Middle-earth.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Carl F. Hostetter (ed.), "Part One. Time and Ageing: XIII. Key Dates", The Nature of Middle-earth, HarperCollins, 2021, pp. 96-98

other names

  • The Gnomish cognate of Inwë is Inwithiel, changed from Gim Githil. His proper (Gnomish) name, according to the Gnomish Lexicon was Inweg or Im, with Githil being the Gnomish cognate of Isil, another one of his names. Githiel was the poetic form of Githil. The original Gnomish form of his name was Ginweg, later changed to Gim or Githil. He was also called Tur yan Eldaron, meaning "King of the Elves" in Qenya, with Tur nan Églathon being its Gnomish equivalent. In the Sketch of the Mythology, from c. 1926, the name Ing is described as being the Gnomish form of Ingwë, as opposed to the earlier conception of Ing being a rejected Qenya form of Inwë.
