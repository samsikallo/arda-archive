etymology

  • Hunthor is given no definition. In The Etymologies, the similar name Hundor was derived from the Noldorin element "hûn" ("heart", as in the physical organ). The element "thor" may be Nandorin, as seen in Denethor.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Lost Road and Other Writings, Part Three: "The Etymologies"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Hunthor”, revid 435456 (2026-02-24T06:54:49Z)

history

  • Hunthor and Dorlas went with Túrin to Nen Girith, where Túrin revealed his plan to climb down the Ravines of Taeglin, cross the river, and ascend the other side so as to strike Glaurung's belly as he stretched across the gap. Dorlas, who had deprecated the other Men of Brethil for lacking courage, could not bring himself to attempt to cross the rock-filled waters. Thus, only Túrin and Hunthor clambered up the west side of the ravine to make their attack.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Children of Húrin, "The Coming of Glaurung"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Hunthor”, revid 435456 (2026-02-24T06:54:49Z)

  • Túrin and Hunthor were nearing the belly of the beast when the heat and stench of the dragon nearly caused Túrin to fall into the river, but Hunthor seized him and saved his life. Túrin proclaimed Hunthor to be of "Great Heart!" but even at the moment a stone struck Hunthor's head and he was lost in the Taeglin.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Children of Húrin, "The Death of Glaurung"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Hunthor”, revid 435456 (2026-02-24T06:54:49Z)

other versions

  • In The Quenta, when Turambar seeks to confront Glómund, he is initially joined by six of his boldest men, but in the night they all slunk away in fear at the rumour of the dragon's arrival, leaving Turambar to confront it alone. In one earlier version of The Grey Annals, Albarth, changed from Albard, was one of Túrin's six companions along with Dorlas. After crossing the Taiglin, Túrin noted that three of his companions were missing and was informed that Albarth fell and was swept away by the Taiglin, while the other two had likely been daunted and slunk away. After emending the tale closer to the final version, where Albarth becomes the one companion who remains with Túrin, his name is changed to Torbarth before becoming Hunthor; in one instance, however, Torbarth was pencilled over with Gwerin. Gwerin is an early Noldorin name (precursor of Sindarin cerin) meaning "enclosure". In his initial genealogy, Hunthor was the son of Hundad and brother of Harathor (Hardang).
