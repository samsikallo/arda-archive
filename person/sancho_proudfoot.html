etymology

  • Jim Allan recognizes the name as one of the short meaningless Hobbitish names in which case it probably represents genuine Hobbitish Sancha. [http://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Sancho Sancho] is a real Spanish name which derives from Latin sanctius "holy". This makes Sancho one of the very few Spanish names appearing in the legendarium.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix F, "On Translation"

history

  • The son of Olo Proudfoot, at the age of eleven, Sancho was present at the Farewell Party of his uncle Bilbo, along with his father and grandfather Odo.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix C, "Baggins of Hobbiton"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Sancho Proudfoot”, revid 396110 (2024-06-25T19:29:52Z)

  • The day after, on 23 September, , he went to Bag End to seek for a treasure he heard rumours about. Frodo could just stop him, but not after Sancho had already started excavating the pantry, where he thought he heard an echo.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "A Long-expected Party"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Sancho Proudfoot”, revid 396110 (2024-06-25T19:29:52Z)

other versions

  • In early drafts of The Lord of the Rings, Sancho is thrown out by Frodo's (at that stage referred to as Bingo) lawyers, Iago Grubb and Folco Burrowes eject him when he tries to loot the pantry.
