What Tolkien Gateway records

history

  • When the Dunlendings invaded Rohan in , Háma took refuge in Helm's Deep with his father. During the Long Winter Helm was besieged, the Rohirrim's food ran low, and the people began to starve. After Yule, Háma led a group out of the fortress on a sortie to find food, but they got lost in a snow storm and never returned.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix A, "The House of Eorl"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Háma (son of Helm)”, revid 429121 (2025-12-08T06:41:22Z)

  • Háma served as the doorguard of Edoras, the abode of the King of Rohan. On 2 March , four guests arrived at his post: Aragorn, Gimli, Legolas and Gandalf. He stood by the rules of the house, and ordered them all to surrender their weapons. He entered into a long argument with Aragorn who refused to hand Andúril to anyone else until he agreed to do so at the wish of Gandalf. He permitted Gandalf to keep his staff, a deliberate decision which would prove decisive in the healing of his king.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix B, "The Great Years"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Háma”, revid 423932 (2025-09-04T23:49:34Z)

  • For this oversight, Háma was reprimanded by Théoden and was briefly relieved of his position as the doorguard. Háma did his Lord's bidding, and also released Éomer from captivity. The King's sister-son had been imprisoned by Gríma Wormtongue, who had been overthrown with the use of Gandalf's staff shortly before. Háma arrested Gríma, and found various missing items in his possession, including Herugrim, the King's sword.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Two Towers, "The King of the Golden Hall"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Háma”, revid 423932 (2025-09-04T23:49:34Z)

  • Háma rode with Théoden to the Hornburg, and fought in the battle that ensued shortly after. He fell before the great gate, and his body was hacked to pieces by a horde of Uruks. He was given the honour of being buried in a grave of his own under the shadow of the Hornburg. Despite his annoyance with his doorguard, Théoden obviously had an affection for Háma, and grieved at his fall, so much so that he personally cast first earth on his grave.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Two Towers, "The Voice of Saruman"; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Two Towers, "The Road to Isengard"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Háma”, revid 423932 (2025-09-04T23:49:34Z)

  • On 5 March when Théoden rejected the words of Saruman, the hewing of Háma's body before the gate of the Hornburg was among the grievances he cited.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Two Towers, "The Voice of Saruman"
