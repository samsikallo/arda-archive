history

  • When Fëanor marched with the Gnomes upon Cú nan Eilch, Gilfanon was one of the Solosimpi who fought against them when he saw his swan-feather boat being stolen. He, along with his sons, threatened the Gnomes, and in the ensuing fight, he was thrown into the sea, but not before uttering a curse.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Shaping of Middle-earth, "I. Prose Fragments Following the Lost Tales: (iii)", pp. 9-10

other versions

  • In The Book of Lost Tales there appears another character called Gilfanon, who was a Gnome of Tavrobel, but Christopher Tolkien is certain that the two are entirely separate characters, and that the Gilfanon of Tavrobel had probably ceased to be named as such at this point in the legendarium.   • His earlier name was Ailios, that was mostly amended in the manuscript of the Tale of the Sun and Moon, but remains in the (earlier) tales given in The Book of Lost Tales Part Two. According to an outline, Gilfanon bids Eriol to write down all the tales that he has heard in Tol Eressëa before drinking limpë. Christopher explains that this and all the details about Gilfanon (like being one of the Noldoli) match with Pengolodh, so Gilfanon would be his literary origin. In a prose fragment following the Lost Tales, there is a Gilfanon mentioned as an Elf of Alqaluntë, but Christopher is certain that he cannot be the Gilfanon of Tavrobel, so probably Tolkien had changed his name.
