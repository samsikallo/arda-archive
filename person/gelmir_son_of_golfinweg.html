etymology

  • The name Gelmir is in Noldorin, but its meaning is unclear. Its Qenya cognate is Nelmer.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, "Sí Qente Feanor and Other Elvish Writings", in Parma Eldalamberon XV (edited by Christopher Gilson, Arden R. Smith, Patrick H. Wynne, and Bill Welden), "Index of Names for The Lay of the Children of Húrin", entries Turgon & Feanor, p. 63

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Gelmir (son of Golfinweg)”, revid 405639 (2024-08-23T03:49:20Z)

history

  • Following his father's death, Gelmir led the Gnomes to the Great Lands. He was slain in the Battle of Unnumbered Tears, and his son Fingolfin died trying to save Fëanor, who was caught and tortured after the battle.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, "Sí Qente Feanor and Other Elvish Writings", in Parma Eldalamberon XV (edited by Christopher Gilson, Arden R. Smith, Patrick H. Wynne, and Bill Welden), "Index of Names for The Lay of the Children of Húrin", entries Turgon & Feanor, p. 63

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Gelmir (son of Golfinweg)”, revid 405639 (2024-08-23T03:49:20Z)

  • Only Turgon, Gelmir's grandson, along with a remnant of his people, as well as the sons of Fëanor, managed to escape from the battle.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, "Sí Qente Feanor and Other Elvish Writings", in Parma Eldalamberon XV (edited by Christopher Gilson, Arden R. Smith, Patrick H. Wynne, and Bill Welden), "Index of Names for The Lay of the Children of Húrin", entry Nínin Unothradin, p. 61

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Gelmir (son of Golfinweg)”, revid 405639 (2024-08-23T03:49:20Z)
