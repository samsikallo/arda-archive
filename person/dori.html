clothing

  • Purple hood and a Silver belt

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Dori”, revid 439057 (2026-06-04T20:06:35Z)

history

  • Dori's parentage is unknown, although he was a member of the House of Durin and a remote kinsman of Thorin Oakenshield. He was also the brother of Nori, and a cousin of Ori. His birth year is also unknown.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, John D. Rateliff (ed.), "Addendum: The Seventh Phase: iv. Personae", The History of The Hobbit, 2011

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Dori”, revid 439057 (2026-06-04T20:06:35Z)

  • However, in Bilbo Baggins' account of the quest to Erebor he stated that the brothers Fíli and Kíli were the youngest of the thirteen "by some fifty years", and that when Thorin was missing Balin was "the eldest left". Since Kíli was born in and Balin was born in , this would put Dori's birth between and c. 2814, and he would have been between c. 127 and 178 years old during the quest to Erebor.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Hobbit, "Over Hill and Under Hill"; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Hobbit, "Barrels Out of Bond"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Dori”, revid 439057 (2026-06-04T20:06:35Z)

  • Thorin stated that Dori was the strongest of the Dwarves in the company. Dori (and Nori) shared Bilbo's opinion that meals should be plentiful and frequent., and also it is said that Dori was "a decent fellow despite his grumbling".

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Hobbit, "Flies and Spiders"; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Hobbit, "Roast Mutton"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Dori”, revid 439057 (2026-06-04T20:06:35Z)

  • When Bilbo first met Dori (in the fourth group of Dwarves to arrive at Bag End alongside Nori and Ori and another set of brothers, Óin and Glóin) he was wearing a purple hood along with a silver belt, same as his brother Nori. During the evening when Thorin called for music, Dori (along with Nori and Ori) played the flute.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Hobbit, "An Unexpected Party"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Dori”, revid 439057 (2026-06-04T20:06:35Z)

  • Bilbo proclaimed Dori to be "really a decent fellow" because thrice Dori assisted Bilbo when the hobbit might otherwise have been killed: First, Dori carried Bilbo in the tunnels of the Misty Mountains during the escape from the goblins, but unfortunately dropped him which led to the hobbit's encounter with Gollum. Upon Nori's discovery that Bilbo was gone the other Dwarves blamed Dori for losing their burglar, especially the wizard Gandalf arguing with the dwarf. Second, during the escape from the Wargs, Dori climbed down from a tree, which allowed Bilbo to scramble up Dori and into the tree. Lastly, Bilbo grabbed Dori's legs as they were carried off by an eagle, saving them from a fire lit by Goblins, much to both Bilbo and Dori's dismay crying "My legs! My poor legs!".

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Hobbit, "Out of the Frying-Pan into the Fire"; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Hobbit, "Over Hill and Under Hill"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Dori”, revid 439057 (2026-06-04T20:06:35Z)

  • When Gandalf was telling the story of the company's adventures to Beorn, Dori and Thorin were the first two Dwarves to show up. Thorin also notes that Dori is the strongest of their company. Upon being pulled out of the barrel after escaping the Elvenking's halls and arrived in Lake-town, Dori, along with several others was one of the Dwarves who was waterlogged and seemed only half alive.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Hobbit, "Queer Lodgings"; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Hobbit, "A Warm Welcome"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Dori”, revid 439057 (2026-06-04T20:06:35Z)

  • Dori would later fight in and survive the Battle of Five Armies. He was given his share of the treasure by Dáin Ironfoot, the new king under the mountain after Thorin's death at the battle by Bolg, and made his home in Erebor alongside his other companions that survived the battle and would live in great wealth under King Dáin.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Hobbit, "The Return Journey"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Dori”, revid 439057 (2026-06-04T20:06:35Z)

other versions

  • On the wet night before meeting the Trolls the prepublication version had Bombur and Bofur failing to light a fire and quarreling about it, with Dori and Nori getting into a fight. In the published version Tolkien added a comment that Óin and Glóin were especially good at making a fire, made them the two who could not do it that night, and had them start fighting.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, John D. Rateliff (ed.), "The Second Phase: Trolls", The History of The Hobbit: Mr. Baggins, 2007, p. 91; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Hobbit, "Roast Mutton"
