history

  • Handir's father, Haldir, joined the Union of Maedhros, and led many Men of Brethil to the Battle of Unnumbered Tears. As the battle turned against Eldar and the Edain, Haldir was slain in the rearguard of Fingon, along with most of the Men of Brethil. Those that survived still had to find their way back through the evil road of Taur-nu-Fuin, and in the end only three Men who had accompanied Haldir returned, healed of their wounds and brought back by Mablung.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Fifth Battle: Nirnaeth Arnoediad"; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Children of Húrin, "The Departure of Túrin"; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The War of the Jewels, "Part Three. The Wanderings of Húrin and Other Writings not forming part of the Quenta Silmarillion: I. The Wanderings of Húrin"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Handir”, revid 421578 (2025-06-27T15:16:11Z)

  • It is not said if Handir was among those who joined the battle, but with the death of his father, he became the leader of the Haladin. News of his father's death also caused his mother, Glóredhel, to fall into grief and die.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Children of Húrin, "The Departure of Túrin"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Handir”, revid 421578 (2025-06-27T15:16:11Z)

  • Around a large group of Orcs crossed the Brithiach. Though Handir resisted them with all the force he could muster, they continued and passed south over the Crossings of Teiglin in search of plunder.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Children of Húrin, "Túrin among the Outlaws"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Handir”, revid 421578 (2025-06-27T15:16:11Z)

  • Starting in the Elves of Nargothrond, under the leadership of Túrin, went openly to battle and drove the Orcs out of West Beleriand; in doing so however they revealed themselves to the wrath of Morgoth. In the spring of , the Orcs invaded the lands of Handir in order to secure the Crossings of Teiglin in preparation for an assault against Nargothrond. Handir gave them battle, but he was slain and his folk driven back into the woods. The Orcs did not pursue them, and continued to muster their strength until their assault against Nargothrond that autumn.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The War of the Jewels, "Part One. The Grey Annals": &sect;272; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The War of the Jewels, "Part One. other versions

  • In Turambar and the Foalókë, Bethos was a stout man and the chief of the wood-rangers with whom Túrin dwelt. As a youth, he had fought in, and escaped from, the Battle of Unnumbered Tears. His wife, "as the tale telleth", was a fair Noldo-maiden and they had many fair sons and daughters save their eldest, Tamar Lamefoot.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Book of Lost Tales Part Two, "II. Turambar and the Foalókë"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Handir”, revid 421578 (2025-06-27T15:16:11Z)

  • When Túrin brought a rescued Níniel back to their settlement, she dwelt in the house of Bethos. Bethos himself was slain not long after in an foray as, despite his years, he still rode abroad. Túrin was then chosen as the new chief of the wood-rangers, rather than Tamar.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Book of Lost Tales Part Two, "II. Turambar and the Foalókë"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Handir”, revid 421578 (2025-06-27T15:16:11Z)

  • In Christopher Tolkien's commentary, he noted the extraordinary statement that Bethos had a Elven wife, and that it contradicted the statement elsewhere that Eärendel alone was a child of both races. Christopher also notes the discrepancy of Bethos being "but a boy" at the Battle of Unnumbered Tears, and considered old ("despite his years") at the time of his death.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Book of Lost Tales Part Two, "II. Turambar and the Foalókë": "Notes and Commentary"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Handir”, revid 421578 (2025-06-27T15:16:11Z)

  • In The Quenta, Handir became the father of Brandir. Handir was the son of Haleth, who in turn was the son of Hador.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Shaping of Middle-earth, "III. The Quenta: Commentary on the Quenta, [Section] 13"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Handir”, revid 421578 (2025-06-27T15:16:11Z)

  • In The Earliest Annals of Beleriand, Handir became the son of Hundor, son of Haleth. Between the years 190-195, Handir allied his people with the Gnomes of Nargothrond who, led by Túrin, drove the Orcs from West Beleriand. He was slain when Glómund and a host of Orcs defeated the forces of Nargothrond.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Shaping of Middle-earth, "VII. The Earliest Annals of Beleriand: [The first version of The Earliest Annals of Beleriand (Text AB I)]"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Handir”, revid 421578 (2025-06-27T15:16:11Z)

  • In a struck-out passage, it was Handir who accompanied Húrin when, by the fate or will of Ulmo, they found the hidden entrance to Gondolin. They were welcomed by Turgon and eventually released after swearing oaths of secrecy of the location of the city.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The War of the Jewels, "Part One. The Grey Annals": &sect;§149–50
