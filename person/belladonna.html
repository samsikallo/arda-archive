etymology

  • belladonna is the name of a poisonous plant, fitting in with the Hobbit-habit of naming girls after flowers. The word is understood as an Italian name, a rarity among Hobbits, and translates as "beautiful lady" although it derives from Latin bladone "nightshade". The name participates in a circular wordplay with the names of her sisters, Mirabella and Donnamira, also Italian names.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Douglas A. Anderson, The Annotated Hobbit, "An Unexpected Party", note 8; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix F, "On Translation"

history

  • Belladonna was one of the three remarkable daughters of the Old Took. She had eight older brothers (and one younger), and was the eldest of the three daughters. Belladonna married Bungo Baggins, who built a spacious hole for her (partly with her money). It became the residence of the Baggins Family, and in , her son and only child Bilbo was born. She died in , eight years after her husband.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Hobbit, "An Unexpected Party"; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix C, "Took of Great Smials"; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix C, "Baggins of Hobbiton"
