history

  • Berúthiel was a Black Númenórean, who was possibly from an inland city that was located to the south of Umbar.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Unfinished Tales, "The Istari", "Notes", note 7, pp. 401-2

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Berúthiel”, revid 437563 (2026-04-17T12:29:14Z)

  • Tarannon's reason for marrying Berúthiel is not known. It is possible that the marriage was in connection with his extensions of the realm of Gondor along the coasts south of the mouths of the Anduin and his victories as the Captain of the Hosts. As a consequence, it is possible that it was a diplomatic marriage to form an alliance with or to maintain peaceful relations with the realm that Berúthiel came from.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix A, "The Númenorean Kings", "Gondor and the Heirs of Anárion", entry for King Tarannon, p. 1044

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Berúthiel”, revid 437563 (2026-04-17T12:29:14Z)

  • Berúthiel loathed the sounds and the smells of the sea, Fish, and the Gulls. She also hated "the house that Tarannon had built below Pelargir" on arches in the water of the mouths of Anduin. As a consequence, she lived in the King's House in Osgiliath instead. Berúthiel hated all colours and elaborate adornments and wore only black and silver clothing. She lived in bare chambers in the house in Osgiliath, but decorated its gardens "with tormented sculptures beneath cypresses and yews".

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Unfinished Tales, "The Istari", "Notes", note 7, pp. 401-2

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Berúthiel”, revid 437563 (2026-04-17T12:29:14Z)

  • Berúthiel loathed cats, but they became attracted to her for precisely that reason. They followed her around, and eventually she took advantage of their company by enslaving and torturing them for her amusement. Of the cats that she kept as her slaves: there were ten of them: nine black and one white. Berúthiel trained the cats to go on evil errands throughout the night to spy on her enemies or to terrify them, in order to discover the dark secrets of Gondor, so that she knew the things that men wished the most to keep hidden. She sent the white cat to spy on the black ones to torment them. Berúthiel was able to converse with them or read their memories. Her cats were infamous among the Gondorians. All were afraid of them, did not dare to touch them, and cursed whenever they saw them.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Unfinished Tales, "The Istari", "Notes", note 7, pp. 401-2

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Berúthiel”, revid 437563 (2026-04-17T12:29:14Z)

  • Eventually, Tarannon exiled Berúthiel from Gondor and her name was erased from the Book of the Kings. He had her set adrift at sea before a north wind on a ship alone that "was last seen flying past Umbar under a sickle moon, with a cat at the masthead and another as a figure-head on the prow" and Berúthiel went back to live in the inland city.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Unfinished Tales, "The Istari", "Notes", note 7, pp. 401-2

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Berúthiel”, revid 437563 (2026-04-17T12:29:14Z)

other versions

  • In an earlier version of what would later become the chapter A Journey in the Dark, it was the narrator rather than Aragorn II who mentioned the cats of Queen Berúthiel. This first version of the sentence referred simply to cats in general with no reference to Berúthiel. The second version of the sentence referred to "the cat of Benish Armon", before the reference was changed to "the cats of Queen Tamar", with a slight revision from Tamar ("Smith") to Margoliantë Berúthiel, before dropping Margoliantë and keeping Berúthiel. Christopher Tolkien did not indicate when the manuscript with the story of queen Berúthiel was written, but J.R.R. Tolkien wrote in a letter to W.H. Auden on 7 June 1955, in a letter to Lord Halsbury on 10 November 1955 and in a letter to Mr. Thompson on 14 January 1956 that he had not yet written the story about the cats of Queen Berúthiel.
