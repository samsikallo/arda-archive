characteristics

  • Known as the wise, she was strong, free of mind, and filled with the desire for knowledge. Though she was strong-willed, she was more patient than her husband. She would seek to understand others instead of master them. Her calmer temperament had passed to some of her sons while others were fiery more like their father. Nerdanel is noted as having brown hair and a ruddy complexion, and not considered among "the fairest of her people." Her father was noted for having hair that was not as dark or black as was that of most of the Noldor, but brown, and had glints of coppery-red in it; three of Nerdanel's children—Maedhros, Amrod, and Amras—had hair of this kind.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Morgoth's Ring, "Part Three. The Later Quenta Silmarillion: (II) The Second Phase: Of Fëanor and the Unchaining of Melkor"; J.R.R. etymology

  • The name Nerdanel is Quenya, but it is given no clear meaning or etymology in the published writings of Tolkien. Editor and linguist Patrick H. Wynne has suggested that the element nerd- in Nerdanel perhaps derives from nerdo ("large, strong man"), noting that the name "might refer to her strength of body and mind, and her pursuits of crafts more commonly practiced by men." Wynne also suggests that Istarnië derives from Quenya ista- ("know"), apparently "referring to her 'desire for knowledge".

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, "Eldarin Hands, Fingers & Numerals and Related Writings — Part One" (edited by Patrick H. Wynne), in Vinyar Tengwar, Number 47, February 2005, p. 33-4

history

  • When Nerdanel married Fëanor, others wondered at his choice for she was not considered among "the fairest of her people." For a time, since Fëanor would seek the counsel of none save her, she was able to influence and restrain her prideful husband.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Morgoth's Ring, "Part Three. The Later Quenta Silmarillion: (II) The Second Phase: Of Fëanor and the Unchaining of Melkor", p. 272; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of Fëanor and the Unchaining of Melkor"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Nerdanel”, revid 421975 (2025-07-09T15:16:38Z)

  • Nerdanel bore Fëanor seven sons: Maedhros, Maglor, Celegorm, Caranthir, Curufin, Amrod, and Amras. Elves typically have four children or fewer, so the size of their family was particularly noteworthy. Some inherited her colors, others inherited her character and patience.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Morgoth's Ring, "Part Three. The Later Quenta Silmarillion: (II) The Second Phase: Laws and Customs among the Eldar, Notes [to Text B]", p. Note 4

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Nerdanel”, revid 421975 (2025-07-09T15:16:38Z)

  • When Fëanor was banished from Túna for drawing a sword against his brother, Nerdanel asked for leave to abide with Indis during that time.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Morgoth's Ring, "Part Three. The Later Quenta Silmarillion: (II) The Second Phase: Of the Silmarils and the Unrest of the Noldor", p. 279

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Nerdanel”, revid 421975 (2025-07-09T15:16:38Z)

  • However, Fëanor's later deeds deeply grieved her and in the end they became estranged from one another.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of Fëanor and the Unchaining of Melkor"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Nerdanel”, revid 421975 (2025-07-09T15:16:38Z)

  • When Nerdanel, who had since returned to her father's house, learned that her husband and sons were planning to go into exile to Middle-earth, she begged Fëanor to leave with her their youngest sons, Amrod and Amras, or at least one of them. Fëanor's response was that, if she would not follow him, she was an untrue wife for deserting both her husband and her children.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Morgoth's Ring, "Part Three. The Later Quenta Silmarillion: (II) The Second Phase: Of Fëanor and the Unchaining of Melkor", p. 354
