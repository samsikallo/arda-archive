etymology

  • "Marigold" (from Mary + gold) refers to the Tagetes annual plants of the daisy family. Tolkien picked the name since it contains "gold" and referring to a golden flower, suggesting the Fallohide strain in Sam's family — which, increased by the favour of Galadriel, became notable in his children.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, "Nomenclature of The Lord of the Rings" in Wayne G. Hammond and Christina Scull (eds), The Lord of the Rings: A Reader's Companion, p. 760

history

  • Like all Gamgees, Marigold was close to the Cotton Family. She married Young Tom Cotton, the eldest son of Farmer Cotton. Marigold's brother, Sam, married Tom's sister Rose.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix C, "The Longfather-tree of Master Samwise"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Marigold Gamgee”, revid 431522 (2026-01-25T18:09:29Z)

  • Sam apparently had a close relationship with her, lamenting the prospect of not seeing her again during his time in Mordor.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Return of the King, "Mount Doom"
