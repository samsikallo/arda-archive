etymology

  • Lenwë is a Quenya word meaning "leaving, departure". It was derived from the ✶ledmē, which included the root LED ("go"). Denwego probably meant "lithe-and-active" from ✶dene- ("thin and strong", "pliant", "lithe") and WEG ("live", "be active").

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, "Words, Phrases and Passages in Various Tongues in The Lord of the Rings", in Parma Eldalamberon XVII (edited by Christopher Gilson), p. 51; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The War of the Jewels, "Part Four. Quendi and Eldar: Author's Notes to Quendi and Eldar", p. note 17; J.R.R. Tolkien, "Words, Phrases and Passages in Various Tongues in The Lord of the Rings", in Parma Eldalamberon XVII (edited by Christopher Gilson), p. 190

history

  • Though Noldorin histories remember him as "Lenwë", his actual name was likely *Denwego, or Denweg in the Nandorin language. He was a Teler of the host of Olwë.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The War of the Jewels, "Part Four. Quendi and Eldar: Author's Notes to Quendi and Eldar", p. note 17; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Morgoth's Ring, "Part Two. The Annals of Aman: Third section of the Annals of Aman", p. §62

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Lenwë”, revid 437528 (2026-04-17T07:20:02Z)

  • Denweg journeyed with the Eldar until reaching the River Anduin, where they established the settlement of Atyamar. Many of the Eldar enjoyed their life at Atyamar, as it was safe and rich in flowers and food. Though their chiefs urged them on, they were unwilling to leave. Eventually, the weather worsened and the winters become hard, and the chiefs gave an order to advance across the Anduin the following Spring; many among the Teleri, however, fought this. When the time came to cross, a great company under the leadership of Denweg, over 3,000 Eldar from the total population of about 28,000, refused and chose to remain at Atyamar. These folk became known as the Nandor.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Carl F. Hostetter (ed.), "Part One. Time and Ageing: VII. The March of the Quendi", The Nature of Middle-earth, HarperCollins, 2021

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Lenwë”, revid 437528 (2026-04-17T07:20:02Z)

  • It was said that many Nandor remained at Atyamar for thousands of years, though others migrated south down the Anduin and settled on the shores south of the White Mountains, especially in later Belfalas. Others went on along the coasts until a number, including Denweg's son Denethor, came up into southeast Beleriand.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Carl F. Hostetter (ed.), "Footnotes", The Nature of Middle-earth, HarperCollins, 2021, p. VII The March of the Quendi, fn2

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Lenwë”, revid 437528 (2026-04-17T07:20:02Z)

other names

  • In earlier versions of the legendarium the character was initially named Dan. This became revised to Dân in the tongue of his people, but he was known as Nano otherwise. The meanings of these names are not glossed.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Shaping of Middle-earth, "VI. The Earliest Annals of Valinor: Notes", p. note 14; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Morgoth's Ring, "Part Two. The Annals of Aman: Third section of the Annals of Aman", p. §62

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Lenwë”, revid 437528 (2026-04-17T07:20:02Z)

other versions

  • In The Later Annals of Valinor, Dan was from a company of Noldoli (Noldor) who forsook the host of Finwë early in the Great Journey and turned south. However, these lands they found were dark and barren due to never having been visited by the Valar, and the sky was scant in stars. After they turned back north, they then marched west with much wandering and grief. Of these people some under [Denithor >] Denethor eventually came to Beleriand. The people of Dan became known as the Danians. This entry was a later addition to the annals, made by Pengolod, as this was not known to Rúmil. In The Lhammas the followers of Dan were briefly emended from Noldorin of the host of Finwë, to Lindarin (Vanyarin) of the host of Ingwë. In the Annals of Aman, Nano's people forsook the journey at the time of the crossing of the Anduin and the crossing of the Misty Mountains. Nano then led his people south down the river and passed out of knowledge of the Eldar. The year for this was 1115. In Quendi and Eldar, his people explicitly rejected crossing the Misty Mountains.
