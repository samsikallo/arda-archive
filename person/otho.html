etymology

  • The name Otho was meaningless in contemporary Hobbitish Westron. It represented an original name Otha.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix F, "On Translation"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Otho Sackville-Baggins”, revid 428122 (2025-11-19T09:14:00Z)

history

  • Otho was the only known child of Longo Baggins and Camellia Sackville. Through his mother, he was the head of the Sackville Family, so he adopted the double surname out of custom. Otho had pipe-weed plantations in the Southfarthing. He married Lobelia Bracegirdle with whom he had a son named Lotho.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix C, "Baggins of Hobbiton"; J.R.R. Tolkien; Humphrey Carpenter, Christopher Tolkien (eds.), The Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien, Letter 214, (undated, written late 1958 or early 1959); J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Return of the King, "The Scouring of the Shire"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Otho Sackville-Baggins”, revid 428122 (2025-11-19T09:14:00Z)

  • He was also second in line to patriarchy of the Baggins Family, and would have been Bilbo Baggins' heir were it not for his adoption of his cousin Frodo. Despite laws dictating that adoption of a member of a different family would not affect headship, Bilbo was of the opinion that Frodo was still deserving of the heirship, as he was a close relative of the same name. Otho objected, though it was ultimately decided that Frodo would become the family's official heir. This did not sit well with Otho and his wife, as they both dearly wanted to live in Bag End.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "A Long-expected Party"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Otho Sackville-Baggins”, revid 428122 (2025-11-19T09:14:00Z)

  • Nevertheless, Otho was a guest at Bilbo's Farewell Party. After Bilbo had vanished in front of the crowd during the celebration, Otho and his wife departed in wrath. The next day he and Lobelia visited Bag End and demanded from Merry Brandybuck to see Frodo and insisted on seeing Bilbo's will, which, unfortunately for him, was valid and clear that Frodo was his legal heir.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "A Long-expected Party"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Otho Sackville-Baggins”, revid 428122 (2025-11-19T09:14:00Z)

  • He did not live long enough to see Bag End as he died in ; it was six years later when Lobelia eventually did come into possession of Bag End, and moved in with her son.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "Three is Company"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Otho Sackville-Baggins”, revid 428122 (2025-11-19T09:14:00Z)
