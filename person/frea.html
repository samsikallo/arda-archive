etymology

  • Fréa means "lord" or "master" in Old English. The element Fréa appears also in the names Fréaláf and Fréawine. Fréan is also the name that Eriol gave to the Valar in his Old English "translation" of the Quenta Silmarillion.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Shaping of Middle-earth, "III. The Quenta: Appendix 1: Fragments of a translation of The Quenta Noldorinwa into Old English, made by Ælfwine or Eriol; together with Old English equivalents of Elvish names"

history

  • Fréa was the great-grandson of Eorl the Young, and succeeded the long-lived Aldor. He was already seventy-five years old when he came to the throne. His fourteen-year reign is recorded as one of peace and prosperity.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix A, "The House of Eorl", "The Kings of the Mark"
