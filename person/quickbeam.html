characteristics

  • Quickbeam was considered very hasty or at least "nearest thing among us to a hasty Ent" according to Treebeard. Part of this hastiness was because he had seen first-hand the actions of Saruman as his home was ravaged by Orcs. Despite this, he was joyous, singing as he walked and laughing easily. He was described by Pippin as gentle but with a fierce hatred towards Saruman for his actions towards his people. Quickbeam appeared to be one of the younger ents. His hair was smooth, his lips ruddy and his hair green. He was tall but could bend and sway in the wind like a slender tree. His voice was resonant and higher and clearer than Treebeard's.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Two Towers, "Treebeard", pp. 482-4; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Two Towers, "Flotsam and Jetsam", p. 568

etymology

  • The name Quickbeam was given to him because he was hasty for an Ent. His Sindarin name, Bregalad, means "quick (lively) tree", derived from *breg- having to do with "violence, suddenness" and galadh, "tree". Quickbeam (Old English Cwicbeám) and Quicken are actual English names of the rowan or mountain ash, Sorbus aucuparia. It was not coincidental that "rowan" is mentioned in Quickbeam's song.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Two Towers, "Treebeard", pp. 482-4; J.R.R. history

  • The rowan-trees in his keeping (among them Carnimírië, Lassemista, and Orofarnë) suffered greatly at the hands of Saruman's Orcs, so he was the first ent to make his mind up at the Entmoot. He sheltered, in his home, the two hobbits, Merry, and Pippin while the other ents made up their minds at the Entmoot. He played a great part in the Ents' resulting attack on Isengard and stopped Saruman when he tried to escape. He was among the Ents guarding Orthanc after the Battle of Isengard.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Two Towers, "Treebeard", pp. 482-4; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Two Towers, "Flotsam and Jetsam", p. 568; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Return of the King, "Many Partings", p. 980
