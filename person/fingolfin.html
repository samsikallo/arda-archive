characteristics

  • Fingolfin was the strongest and most valiant of the sons of Finwë. In the Ancient Quenya of Aman, the spirant s' became voiced as z when between vowels. While this was retained in Vanyarin, it was rhotacized in Noldorin, eventually becoming r. Due to the prestige of the Vanyar, this was change resisted by some of the Noldor (especially those hostile to Fëanor) and the children of Indis. The continued use of the Vanyar (z) pronunciation by the children of Indis, however, was not liked by the general people, even among many who were faithful to Fingolfin & Finarfin and against Fëanor.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of Eldamar and the Princes of the Eldalië"; J.R.R. Tolkien, "Outline of Phonology", in Parma Eldalamberon XIX (edited by Christopher Gilson)

etymology

  • The name Fingolfin is Sindarin, but it is never glossed, although it is said that it is the Sindarized form of the name Finwë Ñolofinwë ("Wise Finwë"). However, in the Noldorin phase of the language, Fingolfin is given as "Magical Skill". In later versions, Tolkien considered changing the name to Ingolfin, as coming from Ingoldo, but the idea was soon discarded.

history

  • Sundered from his kinsmen, and seeing what he believed to be the ruin of his people, Fingolfin rode to Angband in wrath and despair and fell to Morgoth after challenging him to single combat. During the battle Fingolfin wounded Morgoth seven times, and managed one last stab to Morgoth's foot before his death; wounds that would never fully heal. Before Morgoth could feed Fingolfin to his wolves, Fingolfin's body was rescued by Thorondor, the King of Eagles, and brought to a mountaintop overlooking Gondolin where Turgon built a cairn over the remains of his father.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Ruin of Beleriand and the Fall of Fingolfin"

  • Fingolfin led the largest host of the Noldor that departed Aman for Middle-earth. Though against his wisdom, Fingolfin joined with full will in the rebellion and the exile, desiring to go to Middle-earth in order to avenge the murder of Finwë upon Morgoth. Additional reasons were that his son Fingon urged him, he would not be sundered from his people that were eager to go, nor leave them to the rash counsels of Fëanor, and he did not forget his words before the throne of Manwë.

other names

  • His father-name was Ñolofinwë, with the stem ñolo (related to "wisdom") attached to the name of his father. Later, during the Exile of the Noldor, Fingolfin added Finwë to the beginning of this name, in pursuance of his claim to be King of the Noldor after his father's death. This would result in Finwë·ñolofinwë. His mother-name was Aracáno ("High Chieftain"), and was held to be ‘prophetic’. He further gave this name to his son Argon. After Fëanor and Finwë's banishment, a title he was known by was Vinya Finwë ("Young / new Finwë"). Earlier titles in Noldorin were Aran Chithlum ("King of Hithlum") and Taur Egledhrim ("King of the Exiles").
