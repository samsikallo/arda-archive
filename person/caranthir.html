characteristics

  • Physically, Caranthir was notable for two characteristics: his exceptionally dark hair (inherited from his father's side) and his ruddy complexion (inherited from his mother's side). These became the bases of his father-name and mother-name respectively. Caranthir's personality was noted as being harsh, haughty, and quick to anger. His words to Angrod not only diminished the standing of the House of Fëanor at the early council of the Noldor, but remained with Angrod, and the bitterness of them contributed to his confessing the deeds of the Noldor to Thingol many years later.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The War of the Jewels, "Part One. The Grey Annals": &sect;104

other names

  • Caranthir had the sobriquet of "the dark". Caranthir's father-name was Morifinwë ("Dark Finwë"), for he was black-haired as his grandfather. The short form of this name was Moryo. His mother-name was Carnistir ("Red-face") which was a compound of carnë and stîre. In Ælfwine’s translation of the Quenta into Old English; Old English equivalents of Elvish names, his name is given as Colþegn Nihthelm and defined as "[Cranthir the Dark. O.E. col 'coal', þegn 'thane'; niht-helm lit. 'night-helm', but poetically referring to 'cover of night']" An earlier variant of his name was Cranthir (N. "perfect"); it was alternatively spelled as Cranthor.

other versions

  • In The Nauglafring, from the second volume of The History of Middle-earth, the only mention of Cranthor is the attack on Dior's realm to reclaim the Silmaril, and his subsequent death beside Celegorm. In The Lay of the Children of Húrin, from the third volume, Flinding (Gwindor) tells Túrin about the Sons of Fëanor who live secretly in the forests of the East as unflinching foes of Morgoth, including "dark Cranthir". In The Earliest 'Silmarillion', from the fourth volume, an early version has the Sons of Fëanor succeed in recovering the Silmaril after attacking Doriath and slaying Dior; however, owing to the curse of the gold, they then quarrelled for it, until only Maglor was left. This was very soon after emended to Elwing escaping with the Silmaril. In The Earliest Annals of Beleriand, from the fourth volume, the sons of Ulfand (Uldor, Ulfast, and Ulwar) were most beloved by Cranthir among Sons of Fëanor, and they swore fealty to him. In the Battle of Unnumbered Tears, Uldor betrayed the Sons of Fëanor to Morgoth, and fell on their right flank. It was Cranthir who then slew Uldor. In one version of the Battle of Sarn Athrad, the Dwarf army (likely of Nogrod) was defeated and "almost entirely destroyed by Caranthir". While in the published Silmarillion Caranthir is recorded as the fourth son of Fëanor, in The Shibboleth of Fëanor Curufin is explicitly numbered as the fourth. This would then imply Caranthir to be the fifth son instead. In The Nature of Middle-earth, a footnote regarding Finrod's epessë, Felegund, states that "This nick-name was probably actually given to Finrod not by Dwarves but by the Sons of Fëanor at least partly in derision. There was no great love between the Sons of Fëanor and the children of Finarphin, though they hid their enmity at need.
