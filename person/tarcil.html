etymology

  • Tarcil is a Quenya name. Its meaning is not glossed, but the spelling variant Tarkil also appears in early drafts of The Lord of the Rings as the name of the sixth king of Arnor. Based on its etymology, it means "High Man" or "Númenorean" and is a compound of tar- ("high") and a shortened from of Hildo ("Man"). See tarkil for more. In early drafts "the Tarkil" was a nickname for Aragorn before replaced by "the Dúnadan".

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Peoples of Middle-earth, "VII. The Heirs of Elendil", manuscript C with corrections and expansions, The Northern Line of Arnor: the Isildurioni, 6. Tarkil, p. 192.; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Treason of Isengard, "I. Gandalf's Delay", p. 8

history

  • Tarcil ruled uneventfully for eighty years, and was succeeded by his son, Tarondor, upon his death in Third Age 515.
