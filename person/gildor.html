history

  • Gildor was probably the son of some Inglor and one of the Exiles of the House of Finrod. He lived in or near Rivendell. thumb|left|Feasting with the Elves by [[Alan Lee]] Elven Wandering Companies crossed the Shire and Gildor knew well Bilbo and Frodo Baggins when they were out in the woods. Around September , as Bilbo Baggins was leaving the Shire for Rivendell, Gildor met the hobbit as he waved them farewell in one of their camps above Woodhall. The following years he met him once more, perhaps at Rivendell.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "Three is Company", p. 80; J.R.R. Tolkien, "Notes and Translations", The Road Goes Ever On, pp. 65-66; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "Three is Company", p. 83

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Gildor”, revid 409438 (2024-09-08T13:41:22Z)

  • In September , he led a Wandering Company of Elves of Rivendell travelling eastwards from the Emyn Beraid, where they went to see Elbereth in the palantír that was kept there. On the 24th, they were singing, and unknown to him their sounds drove away a Black Rider who was searching for Frodo Baggins, Samwise Gamgee and Peregrin Took on the road near the Woody End. As they passed by them, Gildor was the hindmost, and seeing Frodo he greeted him, to his surprise. Gildor initially intended that the elves bid the hobbits farewell and continue on their way, until Pippin spoke of the Black Riders pursuing them. Upon hearing this, he invited the hobbits to join them, although it was not their custom. He rejoiced after hearing Frodo's Quenya greeting and gave him the title Elf-friend.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "Three is Company", pp. 80-5

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Gildor”, revid 409438 (2024-09-08T13:41:22Z)

  • At night they camped on a hill above Woodhall; they ate and sang, and Gildor discussed the peril that haunted the three hobbits. He sensed Frodo's fears and uncertainties about his travel to Rivendell, and was worried hearing that Gandalf had not returned as he had promised to Frodo. When Frodo asked whether he ought to wait for Gandalf, Gildor replied that the choice was Frodo's, but if he demanded counsel, then Gildor advised him not to wait but to follow Gandalf's plans, with trusty companions. However, he refused to tell him more of the nature of the mysterious Black Riders, except that they were agents of the Enemy, and deadly.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "Three is Company", pp. 80-5

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Gildor”, revid 409438 (2024-09-08T13:41:22Z)

  • After separating from the hobbits, he informed other Wandering Companies to be on the watch, and sent word to the house of Tom Bombadil, probably before the 26th. He also spoke with Aragorn and sent a message to Elrond.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "Three is Company", pp. 80-5; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "In the House of Tom Bombadil", p. 132; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "Strider", p. 172; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "Flight to the Ford", p. 210

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Gildor”, revid 409438 (2024-09-08T13:41:22Z)

  • Two years later, on 22 September , he met the hobbits once again. He accompanied a riding of great Lords and Ladies, that planned to sail West on board the White Ship and probably he was among the Elves who sailed to the West.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Return of the King, "The Grey Havens", p. 1028
