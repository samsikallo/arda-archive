other names

  • Aredhel's Quenya name in Valinor was Írissë correctly rendered in Sindarin as Íreth; Its second element is the feminine suffix -issë simply replaced with its Sindarin equivalent -eth. Paul Strack suggests that her name's initial element seems to be írë "desire" possibly meaning "Desirable Lady". In Ælfwine’s translation of the Quenta into Old English; Old English equivalents of Elvish names, she is called Finhwít; "hwīt" translates to "white".

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Peoples of Middle-earth, "XI. The Shibboleth of Fëanor", p. 345; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Shaping of Middle-earth, "III. The Quenta: Appendix 1: Fragments of a translation of The Quenta Noldorinwa into Old English, made by Ælfwine or Eriol; together with Old English equivalents of Elvish names"

other versions

  • An earlier, Gnomish version of her name was Isfin, which was defined as "snowlocks or exceeding cunning"; it had a Qenya cognate of Iswinde. In a c. 1970 note, Tolkien wrote of this name that "it is now quite unacceptable in form. Unsuitable to the position and character of Turgon's sister, and also meaningless." An epithet applied to this name was "the white-handed". In a rejected note, Isfin was born in 1469, "after the making of the Silmarils, and even as the first shadow fell upon the Blessed Realm". The names Aredhel and Ar-Feiniel ("noble white lady") were both originally intended to stand alone, and be used as the main name of Irissë. While preparing The Silmarillion for publication, Christopher Tolkien could not discover which name was intended to be used as her final name, and he therefore chose to use both names: a decision he later stated was possibly mistaken. Another isolated name in the margin was Rodwen, possibly "High Virgin Noble", which was apparently descarded by Tolkien. While composing the chapter about Maeglin, Tolkien considered Ecthelion, Glorfindel and Egalmoth as the escort of Aredhel in her way to visit Fingon. However, in the published Silmarillion, Christopher didn't mention any of the escorts of Aredhel, based on a note in which his father considers the motives of Celegorm and Curufin in not sending any message to Gondolin about Aredhel. Tolkien decided that it was necessary not to name the most eminent and bravest chieftains as Aredhel's escort, as they would have sought for her beyond the Iant Iaur. In The Grey Annals, Aredhel is recorded as leaving Gondolin in , however Tolkien later rejected that while considering the age of Maeglin: "The date in the Annals is impossible: SY 316. Maeglin would then be about 179 when Tuor came to Gondolin in SY 495; but he would on the 100 : 1 scale be less than 2 years old in life-age!) Gondolin was occupied in SY 116. Isfin therefore cannot have borne Maeglin before, say, 120 – even if she refused entry to Gondolin, not wishing to be “immured”, or escaped soon after Turgon compelled her to enter. So that at best Maeglin would only be 375 years old in SY 495." One of the solutions Tolkien considered while contemplating the age and origin of Maeglin was for him to be born in Aman with his sinister character being "accounted for by the fact that he (and his mother and father) were specially attracted by Melkor, and grew to dislike Aman, and their kin. They joined the host of Fëanor (this would explain Eöl’s skill in smith-craft!) and were estranged from their immediate kin."
