etymology

  • Saruman is a name in the languages of Northern Men, which probably means "Man of Skill". The first element of the name is a moderzined form saru of the Old English word searu ("device", "design", "contrivance", "art" or "skill", "cunning", "cunning device"). The second element is man.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Unfinished Tales, "The Istari", essay on the Istari, p. 390; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Unfinished Tales, "The Istari", "Notes", note 6, p. 401

other versions

  • In the earlier versions of The Lord of the Rings, Saruman was sketched merely as a fawning servant of Sauron, while the army he musters is not in rivalry of Mordor's but from the beginning in his new master's employ. In other drafts, the treacherous Wizard hands Gandalf over to the evil Giant Treebeard or betrays him to the Black Riders. Saruman's older names in these drafts were Sarumond and Saramund, containing the English term mund "hand".

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "The Council of Elrond"
