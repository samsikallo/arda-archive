etymology

  • Théoden is derived from the Old English word þeoden, which means "chief of a people", "prince" or "king". The word þeoden contains the element þeod, which means "people". His epithet Ednew is derived from the Old English word edneowe, which means "renewed". In Tolkien's fictional etymology, the name Théoden is an Old English translation of the original Rohanese Tûrac, an old word for King.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Peoples of Middle-earth, "II. The Appendix on Languages", manuscript F 2, $54

history

  • Théoden was the only son of Thengel, the heir of king Fengel of Rohan, and Morwen of Lossarnach, a descendant of a former prince of the fief of Belfalas. His father left Rohan when he came to manhood and lived long in Gondor. Théoden had been born in Gondor and spent at least the first five years of his life there with his mother and father until his father was called back to Rohan after the death of Théoden's grandfather Fengel.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix A, "The House of Eorl", "The Kings of the Mark"; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Unfinished Tales, "The Disaster of the Gladden Fields", "Appendix: Númenórean Linear Measures"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Théoden”, revid 435372 (2026-02-21T02:47:35Z)

  • Westron, the "speech of Gondor", was used in his father's house in Rohan. Théoden spoke Westron as his native language and he also spoke Rohanese and Sindarin. It is not known if Sindarin and Rohanese were his native languages or his acquired languages. His mother Morwen was a "lady of Gondor of high Númenórean descent". In Gondor all men of high lineage and all those who learned how to read and write used Sindarin, even as a daily language, and it is said that in some families Sindarin was the native language.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Peoples of Middle-earth, "X. Of Dwarves and Men", [Untitled introduction]; J.R.R.   • Théoden loved his sister Théodwyn dearly. After she and her husband both died he adopted her children Éomer and Éowyn as his own. He had a son, Théodred, whose mother Elfhild died in childbirth.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix A, "The House of Eorl", "The Kings of the Mark"
