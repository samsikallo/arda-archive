etymology

  • Freca means "bold man", "warrior" or "hero" in Old English. It is related to the word frec, meaning "bold", "greedy" or "audacious." In Tolkien's translation of Beowulf, he translates freca as "champion."

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien; Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Beowulf: A Translation and Commentary, "Beowulf", 1309

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Freca”, revid 434224 (2026-02-09T05:25:51Z)

history

  • Rohan was invaded from the east in . Sensing their chance, a host of Dunlendings invaded from Isengard and from over the Isen. Their leader was Wulf, who drove Helm into the Hornburg, captured Edoras and sat in Meduseld, and called himself king. Although Helm and his sons died in the Long Winter, his sister-son Fréaláf surprised and slew Wulf and regained Edoras.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix A, "The House of Eorl"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Freca”, revid 434224 (2026-02-09T05:25:51Z)

  • The slaying of Freca was not forgotten by his people and in the War of the Ring, over two hundred and fifty years later, many of them sided with Saruman.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Unfinished Tales, "The Battles of the Fords of Isen", note 4
