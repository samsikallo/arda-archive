What Tolkien Gateway records

Tier 4. Tolkien Gateway is t4: no independent authority. What is carried with each claim is the Tolkien text Tolkien Gateway cited for it.

etymology

  • His name is Quenya. Paul Strack explains it means "Son of the Eldar", being a compound of Eldar and patronymic -ion. In Letter 338, J.R.R. Tolkien spelled this name Eldaron: this is probably a typographic error.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien; Humphrey Carpenter, Christopher Tolkien (eds.), The Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien, Letter 338, (dated 6 June 1972)

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Eldarion”, revid 425188 (2025-09-27T13:12:12Z)

history

  • Eldarion was born in the 1st century of the Fourth Age. He did not inherit the choice of becoming an Elf, but had a long youth like one, having an appeareance of 20 years old until 60.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Carl F. Hostetter (ed.), "Part One. Time and Ageing: XI. Ageing of Elves", The Nature of Middle-earth, HarperCollins, 2021, p. 78

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Eldarion”, revid 425188 (2025-09-27T13:12:12Z)

  • Eldarion became the second King of the Reunited Kingdom after the death of his father Elessar in . At the time of his ascending to the throne, Eldarion was "full ripe and ready for kingship". It is known that Eldarion was given the tokens of kingship (Andúril, etc.) by his father, who died soon after the crowning of his son. He was also recorded to have several sisters.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix A, "The Númenorean Kings", "The Tale of Aragorn and Arwen"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Eldarion”, revid 425188 (2025-09-27T13:12:12Z)

  • During Eldarion's reign, Findegil served as the royal scribe (or King's Writer). In , Findegil wrote a copy of the Thain's Book for the Thain of the Shire, probably at the request of Peregrin Took's great-grandson. This copy was the only one to include Bilbo's complete Translations from the Elvish, which Bilbo had composed while at Rivendell during his 'retirement' there.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, "Prologue", "Note on the Shire Records"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Eldarion”, revid 425188 (2025-09-27T13:12:12Z)

  • It was foretold that he should rule a great realm, and that it should endure for a hundred generations of Men after him, that is until a new age brought in again new things; from him should come the kings of many realms in long days after.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Peoples of Middle-earth, "VIII. The Tale of Years of the Third Age", text of manuscript T4 events after the end of the Third Age in the year 3021

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Eldarion”, revid 425188 (2025-09-27T13:12:12Z)

  • Eldarion's reign as High King of the Reunited Kingdom came to an end in the year of the Fourth Age. Around this time, a dark cult rose to power, but was discovered and defeated that same year. Whether the two events are connected with each other remains unknown.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien; Humphrey Carpenter, Christopher Tolkien (eds.), The Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien, Letter 338, (dated 6 June 1972)

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Eldarion”, revid 425188 (2025-09-27T13:12:12Z)

other versions

  • In the twelfth note to a text written around 1959 and published in The Nature of Middle-earth, it is said that either the slow maturity of the Half-elven, or the "choice of kindred" extended to the third generation, suggesting that Eldarion, and Tar-Amandil, were among the Half-elven. However, this was changed to the second generation in the actual text. In the same text, Tolkien wrote that Eldarion had lived until the age of 125, most likely having passed away in Fo.A. 126. However, it predates the second edition of The Lord of the Rings, where the date of Aragorn's death was ultimately changed by J.R.R. Tolkien from (as in the first edition) to .
