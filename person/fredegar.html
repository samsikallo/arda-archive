etymology

  • J.R.R. Tolkien noted that it was a custom in the Bolger family to use high-sounding names and that he turned them into old names of Frankish and Gothic origin. The name Fredegar contains ferdi, frede ("peace") and gar ("spear"). Fredegar was the name of an author of a Frankish chronicle from the seventh century, known as the Chronicle of Fredegar.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix F, "On Translation", p. 1135

history

  • Fatty was a descendant of Hildibrand Took ( - ), one of the many sons of the Old Took. He was the son of Odovacar Bolger and Rosamunda Took. Fatty was born in and his family was from Budgeford in Bridgefields of the Eastfarthing. He also had a younger sister, Estella Bolger (b. ), who would eventually marry Meriadoc Brandybuck.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix C, "Took of Great Smials"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Fredegar Bolger”, revid 441735 (2026-08-26T14:52:13Z)

  • Fredegar was a good friend of Frodo Baggins. In he was a guest at Bilbo's Farewell Party.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix C, "Took of Great Smials"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Fredegar Bolger”, revid 441735 (2026-08-26T14:52:13Z)

  • Fatty was one of the Conspirators, the friends of Frodo who knew that he was in an important mission to leave the Shire, and they assisted him without his knowledge. Fatty, along with Folco Boffin and Frodo's close circle, in September helped him pack and send his things to Crickhollow, and stayed to celebrate his last birthday before his departure from Hobbiton; Fatty and Merry left the next morning for the house that Frodo had bought, in order to prepare it to be ready and habitable for his arrival.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "Three is Company"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Fredegar Bolger”, revid 441735 (2026-08-26T14:52:13Z)

  • After hearing that Frodo was pursued by the Black Riders, Fatty was given the option to flee with the others into the Old Forest, but that didn't seem any more inviting to him despite Merry's attempts to persuade him that the Riders were far more terrifying than the stories about the Old Forest. Fredegar opted to remain behind as a decoy for the Nazgûl and as a messenger for Gandalf in case he showed up. thumb|left|Christina Wald - Fatty Bolger The Ringwraiths did eventually come in the night of 30 September to Crickhollow for Frodo and the Ring. But Fatty, though frightened nearly witless, escaped to raise the alarm of Buckland, causing the Brandybucks to sound the Horn-call of Buckland. This caused the Nazgûl to flee, but they had learned that the Ring was gone.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "A Conspiracy Unmasked"; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "A Knife in the Dark"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Fredegar Bolger”, revid 441735 (2026-08-26T14:52:13Z)

  • During Saruman's rule of the Shire, Fatty led a group of partisans fighting against the Ruffians in the Brockenbores around the hills of Scary before eventually being captured. He was imprisoned in the Lockholes and starved, (and could no longer be called "Fatty") but was rescued after the victory of the Battle of Bywater.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Return of the King, "The Grey Havens"

other versions

  • In early versions of the text (cf. The Return of the Shadow), his name was Hamilcar Bolger and was rescued by Gandalf from Black Riders before reaching Rivendell, narrating to the protagonists Gandalf's adventures. His larger role was later abandoned. His role survives as a minor anomaly in the published The Lord of the Rings - before the four Hobbits go into the Old Forest, they have six ponies prepared (Fatty was originally supposed to go with them). The number was corrected to five in the 50th Anniversary Edition.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "Note", p. xvii
