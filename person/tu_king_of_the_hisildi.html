history

  • Tû was a wizard, "more skilled in magics than any that have dwelt ever yet beyond the land of Valinor", who became a king among the Ilkorindi, the Elves who never left Palisor. Those Elves that followed him afterwards became known as the Hisildi, and he taught them much lore and wisdom.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Book of Lost Tales Part One, "X. Gilfanon's Tale: The Travail of the Noldoli and the Coming of Mankind", p. 232

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Tû (king of the Hisildi)”, revid 406128 (2024-08-23T04:55:48Z)

  • According to one text, when an Elf called Nuin discovered the sleeping bodies of Men at Murmenalda, Tû subsequently forbade the Elves to wake them or even to visit them, fearing the wrath of Ilúvatar.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Book of Lost Tales Part One, "X. Gilfanon's Tale: The Travail of the Noldoli and the Coming of Mankind", The Awakening of Men according to the earlier outlines, pp. 235-6

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Tû (king of the Hisildi)”, revid 406128 (2024-08-23T04:55:48Z)

  • However, despite this warning, Nuin still visited the sleeping Men, and eventually woke two of them, later called Ermon and Elmir, and taught them his speech. After the awakening of Men, Melko learned of them, and sent his creatures after the Elves and the newly awakened Men, from which Tû protected them.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Book of Lost Tales Part One, "X. Gilfanon's Tale: The Travail of the Noldoli and the Coming of Mankind", The Awakening of Men according to the earlier outlines, p. 236

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Tû (king of the Hisildi)”, revid 406128 (2024-08-23T04:55:48Z)

  • According to another text, Tû eventually "faded before the Sun and hid in the bottomless caverns" after Men "grew in stature".

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Book of Lost Tales Part One, "X. Gilfanon's Tale: The Travail of the Noldoli and the Coming of Mankind", The Awakening of Men according to the later outline, p. 237

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Tû (king of the Hisildi)”, revid 406128 (2024-08-23T04:55:48Z)

other names

  • The character was originally called Túvo, before being changed to Tû. He was also called the Lord of Gloaming.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Book of Lost Tales Part One, "X. Gilfanon's Tale: The Travail of the Noldoli and the Coming of Mankind", p. 234; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Book of Lost Tales Part One, "X. Gilfanon's Tale: The Travail of the Noldoli and the Coming of Mankind", p. 232

other versions

  • In the earlier versions of his story, Tû (called Túvo in that text) was said to have met Melko in the halls of Mandos during Melko's imprisonment, and was corrupted by him. Túvo was said to have learned "much black magic" from Melko, and that after the darkening of Valinor, he went to the Great Lands where he set up a "wizard kingship". However, in later texts, the character of Tû lost these negative connotations, and instead became the protector of Elves and Men.
