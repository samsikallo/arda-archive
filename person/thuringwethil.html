etymology

  • The name Thuringwethil is glossed as "Woman of Secret Shadow" or "she of hidden shadow". The above can be analyzed as thurin ("secret, hidden"), gwath ("shadow") and an obscure ending -il which perhaps denotes feminine gender (and which causes "umlauting" (or "affection") of gwath to gweth). In the earlier versions of the legendarium, Thuringwethil was an Ilkorin name, with Dolwethil being its Noldorin cognate.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Index of Names"; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Lays of Beleriand, "III. The Lay of Leithian: Canto XIII (Beren and Lúthien in Angband)", Note to line 3954, p. 304; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Lost Road and Other Writings, Part Three: "The Etymologies", entry THUR-, p. 393

history

  • She was slain either by Huan or in the collapse of Minas Tirith. Lúthien later used her cloak to sneak into Angband during the Quest for the Silmaril.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of Beren and Lúthien"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Thuringwethil”, revid 440363 (2026-07-18T14:29:59Z)

  • Because of Thuringwethil's ability to change forms, she may have been a Maia or some sort of skin-changer.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Lost Road and Other Writings, Part Three: "The Etymologies", entry THUR-, p. 393

other versions

  • The character of Thuringwethil appears for the first time in the legendarium in the Lay of Leithian. Thus, in The Tale of Tinúviel (the first version of the tale of Beren and Lúthien), Tinúviel simply wears her dark cloak of magic sleep when entering Angamandi.
