etymology

  • Nienna is a Quenya name which contains the word nie ("tear").

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Lost Road and Other Writings, Part Three: "The Etymologies", entry"NEI"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Nienna”, revid 437439 (2026-04-11T15:06:16Z)

history

  • Her part in the Music of the Ainur was one of deep sadness, and from this grief entered the world in its beginning. It was said that her ruth was so great by the discord of Melkor during the Music that she could not endure until it's end.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Valaquenta: Of the Valar"; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), Morgoth's Ring, "Part Two. The Annals of Aman: Commentary on the first section of the Annals of Aman"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Nienna”, revid 437439 (2026-04-11T15:06:16Z)

  • The Maia Olórin, who was later to travel to Middle-earth as Gandalf, learned much from her.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Valaquenta: Of the Maiar"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Nienna”, revid 437439 (2026-04-11T15:06:16Z)

  • Nienna played a part in the making of the Two Trees of Valinor; she wept on the mound of Ezellohar, watering it with her tears. After the destruction of the Trees by Melkor, she once again wept on their wounded remains, cleansing the filth of Ungoliant, and helping to bring forth the last fruit and flower that were to become the Sun and the Moon.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Beginning of Days"; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Flight of the Noldor":"cast back her grey hood"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Nienna”, revid 437439 (2026-04-11T15:06:16Z)

  • The pity of Nienna is most clearly seen in her support for Melkor when he sued for the pardon of the Valar. Though she spent her time in the world mourning for the destruction he wrought in Arda, when he sued for release after his three ages of Captivity, Nienna spoke on his part.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of Fëanor and the Unchaining of Melkor"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Nienna”, revid 437439 (2026-04-11T15:06:16Z)

other versions

  • In The Book of Lost Tales, some early names of Nienna were: Nyenna Fui (Q: "Night"), though this is more correctly the name of her dwelling. Heskil (Qenya: "Winter One") Núri (Qenya: "Sighing One") Qalmë-Tári (Qenya: "Mistress of Death") The importance of Nienna increased along the development of the Legendarium, and even in The Annals of Aman, she is referred to as the sister of Manwë and Melkor.
