etymology

  • The name Tulkas means in Quenya "steady, firm". Tulukhastāz ("the golden-haired") was the original Valarin name for Tulkas.

history

  • Tulkas was created by Eru with the other Ainur and sang in the Song of the Ainur, and chose to enter Eä. His spirit was wandering Ea and did not participate in the formation of Arda with the other Valar: He heard of their First War with Melkor 1500 Valian Years after their arrival, and descended to them from the far heavens. The Dark Lord might have defeated the Valar by himself, but hearing the sound of Tulkas' laughter and beholding his wrath, Melkor fled before him.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Beginning of Days"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Tulkas”, revid 438422 (2026-05-03T11:44:08Z)

  • Thanks to him, the Valar now continued their work in peace, and the Spring of Arda was begun. After the Two Lamps were erected and the Valar had made their first dwelling at Almaren, Tulkas wedded Nessa in a great feast. Being weary and content he slept, and Melkor decided his hour to retaliate had come, and ruined the Lamps, causing a cataclysm. thumb|left|Tulkas by [[:Category:Images by Antonio Vinci|Antonio Vinci]] After the cataclysm, the Valar and their people went to Valinor. Anticipating the Awakening of the Elves Tulkas urged the other Valar to wage war against Melkor.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Beginning of Days"

    [EXT] C? Tolkien Gateway, “Tulkas”, revid 438422 (2026-05-03T11:44:08Z)

  • But when Oromë announced that the first Children of Ilúvatar were awake, Manwë called a council at the Ring of Doom and announced the will of Eru that, for the sake of the Elves, the Valar should regain guardianship of the world from Melkor. Tulkas was glad of this decision. In the Battle of the Powers, after a long siege at Utumno, it was Tulkas who wrestled with Melkor and cast him upon his face. Tulkas chained Melkor with the enchanted chain Angainor, forged by Aulë. For a while the world was free from Melkor's influence.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Coming of the Elves and the Captivity of Melkor"

  • When his sentence was fulfilled, Melkor repented of his actions and was pardoned by Manwë; Tulkas was disappointed with this decision, ever distrustful. But having fought Melkor in the name of authority, he could not rebel himself, and accepted Manwë's decision.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of Fëanor and the Unchaining of Melkor"

  • After the lies spread by Melkor were known, Tulkas and Oromë seached him in the north of Aman, but he was already escaped south. And when Melkor and Ungoliant caused the Darkening of Valinor, Tulkas and Oromë searched in vain for them, unable to penetrate the unlight cast by Ungoliant. Shortly after, Tulkas hurried Fëanor to take a decision about surrendering the Silmarils.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Darkening of Valinor"; J.R.R. Tolkien, Christopher Tolkien (ed.), The Silmarillion, "Quenta Silmarillion: Of the Flight of the Noldor"

other names

  • He was also called Astaldo (Q: "the Valiant"). Astaldo replaced the earlier name Poldórëa. In Eriol's Old English translations, Tulkas is referred as Afodfrea "Strength-ruler". In an early manuscript, a considered surname was: Ender, derived from Enderō ('[?virile] young bridegroom').

other versions

  • In some versions it is said that Tulkas was of the spirits who didn't come into Eä. In other versions, it is said that Tulkas was one of the spirits who entered the vast regions of Eä, until he came to Arda to help Manwë. In The Book of Lost Tales he did not come last and he was young but not the youngest of the great Valar (Ómar Amillo was). Telimektar was his son. In Noldorin, his name was Tolchas.
