etymology

  • Mat was a name employed by Tolkien to translate the common (but untranslated) Hobbit name Matta. The name Heathertoes was, like all surnames of the Men of Bree, a plant name, though this seems to have escaped Tolkien when he wrote a list for translators. In it he said Heathertoes was a Hobbit name, and was given in joke to the unshod Hobbits. While walking, they would gather twigs and moss between their toes. However, Mat was most certainly a Man: given that two of the five were Hobbits, and the last two named victims - Willie Banks and an Underhill were both positively identified as Hobbits as well.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix F, "II: On Translation"; J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Fellowship of the Ring, "At the Sign of the Prancing Pony"; J.R.R. history

  • With the War of the Ring raging in the South-east, many refugees came up the Greenway to Bree. Not all these refugees came with the same intentions: a group of ruffians was ousted from the town near the end of . With the help of Bill Ferny and Harry Goatleaf, they returned soon after New Year, and a fight ensued. The number of casualties on the side of the ruffians was not recorded, but five of the Bree-folk, three Men and two Hobbits, were killed. Mat Heathertoes was one of them.

    Cited to: J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, The Return of the King, "Homeward Bound"
